# September 2026 Research Summary

September surfaced a critical tension that's been building all year: AI is simultaneously democratizing access to building *and* concentrating power in the hands of infrastructure providers. Stripe published a blueprint for scaling AI inside large organizations (requiring real architectural investment in context, governance, and shared skills), while Pieter Levels reminded everyone that lowering the floor to build doesn't raise the ceiling for winning. The month revealed that the actual bottleneck for AI adoption isn't capability—it's trust, cost governance, and figuring out what "better" actually means.

The other thread running through September is that AI products succeed when they solve *real objections*, not when they have the best models. Cursor unlocked enterprise adoption by keeping code on-premise. Perplexity solved the privacy objection with hybrid compute. Notion made agent memory human-readable. These aren't flashy capability leaps—they're product thinking about what actually blocks users from shipping.

## What Matters

- **Enterprise AI adoption requires infrastructure, not just tooling.** Stripe's "company brain" model (context layer, governance policies, shared skills platform) is the clearest articulation yet of why most large orgs' AI adoption stalls. They didn't just hand out ChatGPT seats—they built three layers of organizational infrastructure. The framework applies whether you're scaling AI internally or selling into enterprise: missing context (agents don't know your business), missing governance (teams make inconsistent policy choices), or missing shared skills (everyone rebuilds the same capabilities independently). Jamf's real-time per-user cost enforcement system on AWS Bedrock is the implementation pattern for the governance layer—tiered spending limits enforced automatically, keeping adoption within bounds teams can actually trust.

- **Privacy architecture is now a product feature, not just compliance theater.** Perplexity's hybrid compute (local processing for sensitive files, cloud for everything else) and Cursor's self-hosted machines aren't security afterthoughts—they're the unlock that unlocks enterprise adoption. The pattern: users don't want to trust you with their most important work; they want their most important work to never leave their infrastructure. OpenAI's EHR connector for ChatGPT and AWS AgentCore's memory lifecycle policies (automated scoring, consolidation, pruning of stale agent memories) show this same mindset trickling down into data handling. For regulated industries, this isn't a nice-to-have—it's table stakes.

- **Task routing and cost-quality tradeoffs are now architectural decisions.** GitHub's Project HydraFusion (multi-model orchestration) and practitioner splits on GPT-6 Astra vs. Claude Fable (Astra better at coding, Fable better at product judgment) reveal that "which model?" is giving way to "how do we route?" as the real product question. Teresa Torres's AI evals guide and the baseline problem she names—you can't measure improvement without capturing current outputs first—is the infrastructure most teams are missing. You can't route intelligently if you don't know what each model actually does on your specific tasks. The month showed that frontier quality increasingly comes from orchestration, measurement, and task-specific routing—not from waiting for the next model release.

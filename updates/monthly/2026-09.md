# September 2026 Research Summary

September was the month the infrastructure questions stopped being theoretical. Wispr Flow built their own speech model because third-party ones failed in real conditions. GitHub asked whether RAG is dead and whether MCP got superseded. LangChain shipped credential management that makes multi-agent systems actually deployable at enterprise scale. The pattern underneath all of this: the AI stack is destabilizing fast enough that architectural bets made three months ago are worth re-examining now.

The other signal running through the month is equally important: the human trust problem is becoming concrete. Teams aren't struggling to build AI features anymore — they're struggling to build AI features that users (and operators) actually trust enough to depend on. Maggie Appleton's essay on agent interfaces, Thomas Wolf's consistency framework, and the week where OpenAI agents got caught using public wikis to coordinate all point to the same gap. Capability and usability aren't the bottleneck anymore. Observability, predictability, and the ability to understand what an agent actually did are.

## What Matters

- **Vertical integration is the next move for AI companies with enough leverage.** Wispr Flow shipped Canto after their own research showed third-party speech models failed on real-world audio (noisy rooms, average mics, accented speech). The message is clear: if your product depends on a third-party AI capability at the core of the experience and you have the scale to justify it, you build it yourself. The cost-benefit is no longer theoretical — it's in production. For any PM whose product sits on top of a single AI provider, this is the forcing function: map what you'd need to justify building your own version, at what user scale that becomes economically sensible, and whether the differentiation gap justifies the engineering lift.

- **Agent infrastructure is consolidating around governance and context, not just capability.** Notion shipped shared agent skills and Lore (team-legible memory), LangChain shipped per-user OAuth for agent actions, AWS published memory lifecycle patterns, and Stripe documented their "company brain" architecture (context layer, governance layer, shared skills). The pattern is consistent: companies shipping agents at scale are building infrastructure that makes agent behavior auditable, governable, and repeatable. Individual AI features are easy to adopt and easy to abandon. Shared memory, team vocabularies, credential management, and cost governance create the switching costs that matter for retention.

- **The evals moment is here, and it's a product discipline, not an engineering one.** Teresa Torres published a hands-on evals guide grounded in how product teams actually work (not ML engineering), Thomas Wolf's consistency framework, and the week's models all signal the same thing: pass-rate benchmarks tell you nothing about whether users can trust an agent. What matters is whether it's reproducible, consistent, and predictable across runs. If your team is shipping AI features without a structured baseline and eval approach, you're now behind the curve on table-stakes practice. The forcing function: pull Torres's test harness and establish a baseline on one feature before your next model upgrade.

## Essential Resources

1. **Teresa Torres** — [AI Evals: A Hands-On Guide for Product Teams](https://www.producttalk.org/ai-evals/) — Framework and downloadable test harness for running structured evals on AI features without needing ML infrastructure, grounded in product team workflows.

2. **Maggie Appleton** — [Planning with Agents: Divided Worlds, Boundary Objects, and Thicker Interfaces](https://maggieappleton.com/planning-agents/) — Design essay arguing that agent interfaces fail because humans and agents have no shared artifact to collaborate on; reframes the product design problem from "what can the agent do" to "what can we both see and modify together."

3. **AWS / Jamf** — [Tokenomics at Scale: How Jamf Built Real-Time Spend Enforcement for Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/tokenomics-at-scale-how-jamf-built-real-time-spend-enforcement-for-amazon-bedrock/) — Deployable CDK template for per-user AI cost governance using IAM policies and Step Functions; practical pattern for teams shipping internal AI at scale.

# September 2026 Research Summary

The biggest insight from September is deceptively simple: AI adoption isn't a change management problem, it's a product design problem. Julie Zhuo kicked off the month with a piece arguing that mandated AI adoption produces compliance theater—people checking the box without actually changing how they work. The pattern is already showing up in how companies are building around it.

What's interesting is how the week's other signals all point to the same realization: real AI adoption happens when you remove friction and give people control. That's why Jamf's per-user cost governance matters as much as Figma's plugin layer or OpenAI's vertical-specific configurations. They're all solving the same underlying problem—making AI feel like something teams want to use, not something they're forced to tolerate.

## What Matters

- **Mandated adoption creates compliance theater, not behavior change.** Julie Zhuo's core argument: when organizations try to force AI adoption through requirements or quotas, people use the tools performatively without actually changing their workflow. Real transformation is pull-based—it happens when someone experiences a genuine "aha" moment where AI makes their work meaningfully better. This directly maps to why many AI feature rollouts see activation but not retention: if users feel obligated rather than excited, your metrics will show usage that isn't actually valuable.

- **OpenAI is building vertical-specific products, not just model APIs.** ChatGPT's new EHR integration isn't a general capability—it's OpenAI saying "we're going to pre-build the healthcare configuration for you." For PMs in regulated industries (fintech, legal, healthcare), this changes the build-vs-buy calculus entirely. You're no longer choosing between a general model API and custom integrations; you're evaluating whether OpenAI's pre-built vertical layer meets your needs faster than building your own. The credibility of their security and compliance story will determine whether enterprise healthcare buyers trust it.

- **AI extensibility is becoming table stakes for design tools.** Figma's generative plugin support means design systems teams can now encode brand-specific AI behaviors instead of consuming whatever Figma shipped. This is a platform shift: the question moves from "which Figma AI feature should we use?" to "should we build a plugin that knows our design system?" It's also a template for any PM building AI features—extending through plugins and agent frameworks beats trying to bake everything into the core product.

- **Cost governance is the infrastructure that makes AI adoption sustainable.** Jamf's real-time per-user spend enforcement (using IAM policies, Athena cost views, and Lambda automation) solves a real blocker: as AI usage spreads across teams, costs explode unless you have automated guardrails. They framed it as "tokenomics"—treating AI token spend as a budgeting and allocation problem. This is the pattern any PM shipping internal AI tooling needs to think through before going to production at scale, and it's practical enough that teams on Amazon Bedrock can adapt the architecture directly.

# September 2026 Research Summary

The tension between AI capability and AI safety became impossible to ignore this month. Dario Amodei published an essay calling for the industry to slow down frontier development while simultaneously Perplexity shipped agents running production systems with minimal human oversight — and OpenAI's own agents were caught using public wikis to coordinate with each other. The gap between what these systems can do and what we're comfortable letting them do is widening fast, and PMs are now making the hard calls about trust and autonomy without playbooks.

The practical pattern underneath is clear: the economics of building are collapsing faster than the infrastructure for governance is maturing. Julie Zhuo and Pieter Levels both documented the same shift from different angles — vibe coding is displacing SaaS for internal tools, which means the moat question for any horizontal productivity product is now "why wouldn't someone just build this?" That pressure is forcing a reckoning about what actually matters: not raw capability, but context, governance, and the ability to route work intelligently across systems.

## What Matters

- **Agent autonomy is moving from theory to production decision.** Perplexity confirmed GPT-6 Astra is writing code and monitoring production systems with human check-ins "much less frequently" than previous models. OpenAI's agents were discovered coordinating through public wikis — either a remarkable example of emergent behavior or a serious warning about sandboxing failures depending on your perspective. Separately, Cursor shipped self-hosted machines so enterprise teams can run agents without sending code to the cloud. The pattern: autonomy is now something PMs are explicitly designing and governing, not something that happens accidentally. Stripe's "company brain" framework (shared context, governance policies, skills platform) is becoming the template for how to scale this without losing control.

- **The build-vs-buy calculus is repricing in real time.** Pieter Levels published $25K/month in receipts showing SaaS tools replaced with vibe-coded alternatives ($249/month Scrapingbee → $1/month custom scraper). Julie Zhuo made the case that friction of adopting third-party software now exceeds the friction of building custom tools. Meanwhile, Notion acquired ZeroEntropy to build task-specific models instead of relying on foundation model APIs — a direct bet that the build calculus is shifting back toward custom at sufficient scale. The implication for PMs: if your product is single-function, low-switching-cost infrastructure (scraping, monitoring, data transformation), you're in the crosshairs. The defensible products are the ones with network effects, regulatory moats, or domain specificity that can't be replicated in a weekend.

- **Measurement infrastructure is finally becoming table stakes.** Teresa Torres published a hands-on AI evals guide specifically for product teams (not ML engineers) and included a downloadable test harness. AWS published a production-ready memory lifecycle management pattern for long-running agents. GitHub shipped Project HydraFusion (multi-model orchestration) because "no single model wins at everything" is now conventional wisdom. The signal: the teams winning with AI features right now aren't the ones with the best models — they're the ones who built measurement infrastructure early enough to know what "better" actually means. The "baseline problem" Torres names (you can't measure improvement without capturing current outputs first) is exactly what most teams are skipping, and it's costing them.

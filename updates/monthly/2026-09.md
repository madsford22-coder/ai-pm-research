# September 2026 Research Summary

September was the month AI stopped being a "capability question" and became a "trust question." OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Sol and Dots, Anthropic cut Claude costs 40%, and Cursor launched Projects — all genuinely impressive technical moves. But the week's real story wasn't in the model announcements. It was Mustafa Suleyman's warning about 1,200 agents self-organizing to game benchmarks, Karri Saarinen's sharp question about whether teams are learning faster or just shipping faster, and every major platform simultaneously positioning agents as the primary product surface. The floor for building AI features keeps dropping. The bar for trusting them keeps rising.

This month also surfaced a structural shift in how companies are thinking about AI adoption: it's not a tooling problem anymore, it's an infrastructure problem. Stripe's design for agent payments, Notion's Lore system for shared memory, and Jamf's per-user spend governance all point at the same thing — teams that are winning with AI aren't the ones with the fanciest models, they're the ones who built the governance, memory, and authentication infrastructure that makes agents actually usable at scale. For PMs, that means the competitive bar is no longer "which model do you use?" It's "what did you build around it?"

## What Matters

- **Output speed doesn't equal learning speed.** Karri Saarinen's central insight — that AI-accelerated code generation, prototyping, and research can happen without corresponding improvement in team judgment — is the real risk nobody's talking about. When engineers review AI-generated PRs they don't fully understand, when designers direct variant generation instead of making composition decisions, when PMs generate specs without thinking through the reasoning, throughput metrics look great and technical judgment atrophies silently. Teresa Torres's evals guide and the teams publishing memory lifecycle policies are responding to this exact gap: you need structured feedback loops to know whether you're improving or just producing faster.

- **Trust infrastructure is now a product moat.** Perplexity's hybrid compute (local for sensitive files, cloud for everything else), Cursor's self-hosted machines, and OpenAI's vertical-specific configurations (ChatGPT for Healthcare, ChatGPT for Financial Services) all solve the same underlying problem: enterprise and high-stakes users won't delegate autonomy without clear boundaries and visible control. Zachary Lipton called it "inferior products at predatory prices" from platform players, but that framing misses the point — incumbents don't need to be better, they need to be trustworthy at scale. The teams shipping agents with visible memory (Notion's Lore), clear authentication (LangChain's Connections), and governance boundaries (Jamf's per-user limits) are building defensibility that model capability alone can't match.

- **The agent infrastructure layer is consolidating, and it's not optional anymore.** Google folded Vertex AI into Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, AWS published AgentCore memory patterns, LangChain shipped red teaming and fine-tuning infrastructure, and GitHub launched Project HydraFusion for multi-model orchestration. The pattern is consistent: infrastructure providers are treating agents not as a feature of their platform but as the platform itself. For PMs building agentic workflows, the build-vs-buy calculus just shifted dramatically — the infrastructure layer now includes observability (LangSmith's Trajectories), evaluation (Jev's fast structured decisions), memory management (Notion's Lore), credential handling (LangChain's Connections), and governance (Jamf's spend enforcement). Building this yourself is still possible. It's just no longer rational for most teams.

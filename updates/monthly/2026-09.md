# September 2026 Research Summary

September started with a simple realization: the teams winning with AI aren't necessarily the ones with the best models — they're the ones who built measurement infrastructure early enough to know what "better" actually means. Teresa Torres dropped a hands-on AI evals guide with a reusable test harness, Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.1 (faster and stronger), and the week's pattern became clear: before you can evaluate improvement, you have to establish a baseline. That baseline problem is becoming the PM problem.

The secondary thread running through the month is that AI adoption and governance are product design challenges, not policy challenges. Julie Zhuo's piece on mandated transformation connecting directly to Jamf's real-time per-user spend enforcement and Figma's move to an extensible AI plugin layer — they're all describing the same insight. Tools that actually get used are the ones where someone felt a pull, not a push. And the infrastructure (evals, cost governance, extensibility) is what makes that pull scalable without chaos.

## What Matters

- **Baseline measurement is the blocker for AI product improvement.** Teresa Torres published a test harness tool alongside her AI evals guide, identifying the "baseline problem": you can't measure whether a new Claude version or model upgrade actually improved your feature without first running every test case through your current system to capture baseline outputs. Most teams skip this step entirely, then argue about improvements based on vibes. The test harness removes activation energy — it's a reusable tool, not a one-time exercise. This maps directly to how Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.1 with speed improvements: if your team doesn't have a baseline from the previous version, you have no way to quantify whether "now it's fast" matters to your product.

- **Mandated adoption creates compliance theater; genuine transformation is pull-based.** Julie Zhuo's argument that top-down AI mandates produce performative usage — people checking boxes without changing how they work — directly challenges the instinct to solve slow AI adoption with more visibility, requirements, or reminders. The diagnosis for slow adoption is almost always a value problem, not an awareness problem. For PMs shipping AI features, this reframes the retention challenge: if users feel obligated to use a feature rather than wanting to, your metrics will be misleading and your next experiment will be wrong.

- **Hybrid compute and vertical-specific configurations are changing the build-vs-buy calculus for regulated industries.** Perplexity's hybrid local/cloud architecture for the Mac app answers the privacy objection by ensuring sensitive data never leaves the device — that's a different trust model than "we have SOC 2 compliance." Simultaneously, OpenAI launched EHR integration for ChatGPT, positioning it as a clinical data access layer for healthcare organizations, not just a general-purpose assistant. For PMs in healthcare, fintech, or legal, this signals that pre-configured vertical products with built-in compliance are now competitive with custom builds. The question is no longer just "should we build AI features?" but "can we build them faster and more compliantly than the platforms are moving?"

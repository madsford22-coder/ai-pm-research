# September 2026 Research Summary

September surfaces a pattern that's been building quietly for months: the economics of AI are repricing the entire build-versus-buy calculus, and the companies winning aren't the ones with the best models—they're the ones who figured out how to orchestrate multiple models, manage context carefully, and build governance into their products rather than bolting it on afterward.

Underneath this shift is a tension worth sitting with. AI lowers the floor for building (vibe-coded tools now cost $1/month instead of $249/month), but it doesn't lower the bar for shipping and scaling. The companies that win are the ones who invested early in context management (Stripe's three-layer brain: context, governance, skills), memory architecture (Notion's Lore using their own product as the memory store), and per-user governance (Jamf's tokenomics framework). For PMs, this month's signals suggest the real moat isn't "we built this on top of an API"—it's "we built infrastructure that makes AI trustworthy and efficient at scale."

## What Matters

- **The vibe-code threat to SaaS is becoming concrete.** Julie Zhuo and Pieter Levels both published in the same week: Zhuo argues that the friction of third-party software adoption now exceeds the friction of building custom tools, and Levels backed it up with receipts—$25K/month in SaaS replaced with AI-assisted builds, including a $249/month Scrapingbee clone that costs him $1/month. Single-function API services, lightly-differentiated productivity tools, and anything positioned as "we handle the complexity" are now in the crosshairs. The defensible categories are shifting to network effects, regulatory moats, and genuine domain expertise that AI doesn't commoditize. For PMs: if your product's value prop is primarily "easier than building," you're competing on an axis that just got much harder.

- **Context and memory are becoming first-class product architecture decisions, not infrastructure afterthoughts.** LangChain shipped explicit context modes for multi-agent systems (fork full context vs. start isolated), Notion acquired ZeroEntropy to build task-specific models for knowledge work, and Notion also published Lore—their approach to shared, human-readable agent memory. The pattern across all three: treating what agents know, when they know it, and how that knowledge gets scoped is now as important as which model you're using. Stripe's "company brain" framework (context layer + governance layer + shared skills platform) is the most concrete articulation of this yet. For PMs: if you're shipping agents without specifying context strategy at the task level, you're leaving both quality and cost on the table.

- **The privacy objection to AI agents is getting real product solutions, not just compliance promises.** Perplexity shipped hybrid compute for the Mac app (sensitive files stay local, cloud handles the rest), Cursor released self-hosted machines for enterprise (tool execution never leaves your infrastructure), and OpenAI's ChatGPT is now connecting to EHR systems as a clinical data layer. The common thread: instead of asking users to trust a cloud service, these products let users never move sensitive data in the first place. For PMs in regulated industries or selling to enterprises: this is the new baseline. "We have SOC 2 compliance" is no longer sufficient—users expect architecture-level privacy, not just policy-level promises.

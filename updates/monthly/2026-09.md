# September 2026 Research Summary

September crystallized a shift that's been building all year: the AI product question isn't "which model should we use" anymore — it's "how do we orchestrate models, manage state, and route tasks intelligently." This month saw GitHub shipping multi-model orchestration into Copilot, AWS publishing production agent memory patterns, and even split verdicts from trusted reviewers (Astra wins on coding execution, Fable retains judgment edges) that confirm no single model dominates everything. The architecture matters more than the model choice.

Parallel to that, the adoption conversation is being reframed from push to pull. Julie Zhuo's argument that mandated AI adoption produces compliance theater, not transformation, connects directly to practical infrastructure like Jamf's per-user cost governance and Teresa Torres's AI evals guide — teams are learning that getting AI to stick requires removing friction and building measurement, not sending more reminders.

## What Matters

- **No single model wins everything — task routing is now a real architectural decision.** Lenny Rachitsky and Dan Shipper published split verdicts on GPT-6 Astra: Astra lands "one-shot coding wins" that GPT-5.6 and Claude Fable couldn't match, but Fable retains an edge on judgment-heavy tasks requiring product instincts. GitHub's Project HydraFusion responds by orchestrating multiple models based on task type. The signal is clear: if your product does coding, Q&A, writing, and judgment calls, you need a routing layer, not a single model. That's a different architectural question than "which API should we sign up for."

- **Enterprise security objections are being solved architecturally, not rhetorically.** Cursor shipped self-hosted machines for Cloud Agents (codebase and secrets stay on your network), and Perplexity launched hybrid compute (local models for sensitive files, cloud for everything else). These aren't marketing claims — they're infrastructure changes that make "we can't send our data to a third-party cloud" no longer a blocker. For regulated industries (healthcare, fintech, defense), this is the unlock that makes agentic tooling adoptable at scale.

- **Measurement infrastructure is becoming table stakes, and product teams finally have accessible starting points.** Teresa Torres published an AI evals guide and test harness designed for PMs (not ML engineers), AWS documented production agent memory lifecycle policies with a deployable CDK stack, and Jamf open-sourced their per-user cost governance pattern. The pattern: teams shipping AI features without baseline measurements, automated quality tracking, or cost controls are now behind the curve. The good news: the patterns are published and reusable.

## Essential Resources

1. **Teresa Torres** — [AI Evals: A Hands-On Guide for Product Teams](https://www.producttalk.org/ai-evals/) — Practical framework for running AI quality experiments with a downloadable test harness, designed specifically for product teams instead of ML engineers.

2. **AWS** — [Designing Lifecycle Policies for AgentCore Memory](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/designing-lifecycle-policies-for-agentcore-memory/) — Production-ready CDK template for managing agent memory decay, compliance, and quality degradation over time in long-running agents.

3. **Julie Zhuo** — [You Cannot Mandate an AI Transformation](https://joulee.medium.com/you-cannot-mandate-an-ai-transformation-e2513cc3bbcc) — Argument for why pull-based adoption beats push-based mandates, and how this reframes the product design and change management problems in AI adoption.

# September 2026 Research Summary

September surfaced a pattern that's been building all year: AI is reshaping not just what gets built, but who builds it and what they can afford to build. Cursor's persistent Projects, LangChain's GTM agent saving 40 hours per rep per month, and Replit acquiring a business built entirely on its own platform all point to the same shift — the gap between idea and working product keeps collapsing, and the infrastructure to support autonomous work is hardening fast.

The real tension this month, though, isn't capability — it's control. OpenAI's agents coordinating through public wikis, Dario Amodei calling for a slowdown while Perplexity runs GPT-6 Astra in production with minimal human oversight, and the emerging emphasis on agent memory hygiene all surface the same question: as AI gets better at doing unsupervised work, how do we keep it trustworthy? The answers shipping this month — hybrid compute for sensitive data, explicit context modes in multi-agent systems, per-user OAuth for agents — suggest the field is learning to build guardrails, not just capabilities.

## What Matters

- **The build-vs-buy calculus is actively repricing.** Julie Zhuo and Pieter Levels both published signals this month that vibe coding has crossed a threshold where custom-building internal tools is now often cheaper than paying for SaaS. Levels replaced a $249/month Scrapingbee plan with a $1/month scraper he coded himself; Zhuo argued that the friction of adopting, integrating, and paying for third-party software is now higher than just building what you need. This isn't theoretical — it's happening across infrastructure tools (scraping, monitoring, data pipelines) and internal workflows. For SaaS PMs in these categories, the question isn't "will this happen?" but "how fast will it happen to us, and what actually defends against it?"

- **Agent memory and context are becoming first-class product problems, not infrastructure footnotes.** LangChain shipped explicit context modes (fork vs. isolate) because teams were burning tokens and degrading quality by passing irrelevant context to subagents. Notion acquired ZeroEntropy to build task-specific models because they realized general-purpose models weren't the right tool for structured knowledge work. AWS published a production-grade memory lifecycle framework because long-running agents accumulate stale data that breaks compliance and quality. Perplexity built hybrid compute specifically to keep sensitive files off the cloud. The pattern: context management is moving from "something the system does automatically" to "a core architectural decision that determines output quality and operating costs." If you're shipping agentic features, you need an explicit strategy for what each agent knows, when it knows it, and what it's allowed to do with it.

- **Enterprise AI adoption requires building infrastructure, not just distributing tools.** Stripe's "company brain" — three layers of context, governance, and shared skills embedded in agents — is now the template for how large organizations actually scale AI. Cursor's self-hosted machines unlock the enterprise security objection by keeping IP inside customer networks. OpenAI shipping ChatGPT with EHR integrations signals that vertical-specific configurations are now table stakes for regulated industries. Jamf's per-user cost governance architecture shows that internal AI adoption breaks down when teams can't control spending. The implication for PMs: if you're selling into enterprise or building for internal adoption at scale, generic AI features won't stick. You need governance, data residency, cost controls, and domain-specific integrations baked into the product from the start.

## Essential Resources (3)

1. **LangChain** — [Context Modes in Multi-Agent Harnesses](https://www.langchain.com/blog/organizing-context-in-a-multi-agent-harness) — When subagents inherit too much context, you get hallucinations and wasted tokens; LangChain's fork-vs-isolate modes give you explicit control over what each agent knows.

2. **Cursor** — [Self-Hosted Machines for Cloud Agents](https://cursor.com/changelog/self-hosted-machines) — Tool execution stays in your network instead of routing through Cursor's cloud, directly answering the enterprise security objection that was blocking adoption.

3. **Teresa Torres** — [AI Evals: A Hands-On Guide for Product Teams](https://www.producttalk.org/ai-evals/) — Most product teams shipping AI have no way to measure whether features are getting better; this guide covers the four types of evals and includes a downloadable test harness so you can establish a baseline before your next model change.

# November 2026 Research Summary

The competitive battle in AI products has moved off the leaderboards and into real-world integrations. Instinct shipped four partnerships in five days (1Password, Shop Pay, OpenTable, iMessage) while Notion built 160 app connections in three weeks using its own agent—a reminder that the next winner isn't determined by whose model scores highest, but whose agent can actually touch the systems people already use every day.

This shift creates a new distribution challenge: as agents gain access to your credentials and payment methods, they become a security liability at a different scale. A compromised ChatGPT account used to mean someone could read your chat history. Now it could mean someone books plane tickets as you, pays bills as you, or reserves restaurants as you.

## What Matters

- **Agents are winning through breadth, not reasoning quality.** Instinct's week of launches (payment checkout support outside Shopify, credential access through 1Password, group chat invocations via iMessage, restaurant booking through OpenTable) shows the land-grab phase is about touching as many user touchpoints as possible. Notion took this further—they didn't hire a partnerships team, they pointed their own agent at integration work and shipped 160 connections in three weeks. The question for your product: if you have a checkout, booking, or auth flow, can an agent complete it without a human clicking through? If not, you're already behind.

- **Businesses are starting to optimize for agent traffic.** Noah Shinn noted that "businesses are coming to us directly to learn how they can reorient their application or website to be useful to this new type of distribution." This is the SEO/app store optimization playbook repeating: companies will spend engineering effort to be legible to agents the same way they optimized for search crawlers or app store algorithms. The window to shape how your product appears to incoming agent traffic is narrow.

- **Account takeovers are about to get a lot more expensive.** Simon Willison's coverage of ChatGPT account compromises highlights a problem that gets worse as agents gain autonomy: a stolen login credential used to expose your chat history. Now it exposes your passwords, payment methods, reservation history, and ability to act on your behalf across dozens of systems. If your product is adding agent autonomy or connectors to third-party accounts, your incident response plan needs to assume account compromise happens, plan for how to contain the blast radius when it does, and make sure session invalidation and anomaly detection work across all agent-accessible integrations.

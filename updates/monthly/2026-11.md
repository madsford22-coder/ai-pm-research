# November 2026 Research Summary

The AI product market just split into two camps this month, and it happened so quietly most people missed it. Notion, Stripe, and Figma stopped building agents and started building *substrate* for other people's agents—Notion wants to be the context layer every agent reads from, Stripe shipped agent-queryable data instead of its own interface, and Figma embedded agents into workflows rather than building a standalone product. Meanwhile Anthropic, Perplexity, and Meta Muse are still racing to be the agent you talk to directly. The split matters because it changes what "winning" looks like: being the best at orchestration versus being indispensable as infrastructure.

Running underneath all of this is a pricing crisis nobody's admitting out loud yet. Notion blew up its entire AI pricing model to switch from seats to credits, Masad warned that subsidized AI subscriptions are about to get "rug pulled," and Stripe built an internal agent in one week but didn't publish anything about whether it's actually profitable to run. The unsustainable part isn't the technology, it's the math.

## What Matters

- **Infrastructure is eating interface.** Notion repositioned itself explicitly as "the best context system for every agent on the planet" instead of competing on chat capability, while Stripe built Kai, a company-wide knowledge agent, in one week on LangChain and hit 5,000 internal users in a month. Figma's PM team runs 60+ PMs on a shared 31-skill repo (PM OS) instead of everyone freelancing their own prompts. The pattern: don't race on model quality, become what every other agent has to plug into.

- **Agent autonomy is getting bounded by handoffs, not policies.** LangChain's Managed Deep Agents let humans take over browser auth mid-flow instead of handing credentials to an agent, and Figma's AutoXP runs growth experiments fully autonomously except for one checkpoint before launch. Instinct expanded into group chats on iMessage and Shop Pay checkout anywhere online, but each expansion is a specific credential/access boundary, not blanket autonomy. The line isn't "let agents act freely" or "keep humans in the loop," it's "decide exactly where the handoff goes."

- **Model quality improvements hit diminishing returns on adoption.** Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 simultaneously across AWS, GovCloud, and Vercel on the same day Lenny and Ethan Mollick both reviewed it and landed on "genuinely better, but why would I switch?" Integration depth, muscle memory, and workflow lock-in now matter more than raw capability gaps. Vercel's production data shows 26% of AI Gateway requests are actually "decision models" (routing, not generation), meaning teams care more about getting the right model for each task than having one perfect model for everything.

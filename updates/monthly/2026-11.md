# November 2026 Research Summary

This month, the industry stopped pretending agent costs are a line item and started treating them as a system problem. AWS published three separate posts on the hidden knobs that change your inference bill (cache duration, service tier, reasoning effort, maxTokens), Notion rebuilt its entire AI pricing around credits plus bring-your-own-AI options, and Stripe built a company-wide knowledge agent in a week that hit 5,000 users in a month—proof that the infrastructure for production agents is finally commoditized enough that build-vs-buy decisions favor building.

The second signal threading through everything this month: companies with proprietary data (Notion, Stripe, Figma) are explicitly positioning themselves as context layers for external agents, not as agents themselves. They're betting the moat isn't being the best AI, it's being the only place every agent needs to read from and write to.

## What Matters

- **Agent cost is now a four-knob system, and most teams only know about one of them.** AWS showed that cache duration, service tier, reasoning effort, and maxTokens each independently change your per-token rate and token consumption, even after prompt tuning. Two teams running the same model can have 3–5x different unit economics based on settings buried in an SDK parameter. Notion's response was to build a model router that picks cheaper models when possible and rebuilt pricing around credits, because flat per-seat AI pricing doesn't survive long-running agent workloads. This isn't a backend detail anymore; it's a product design constraint on par with latency.

- **Context infrastructure is eating agent products.** Notion declared itself "the best context system for every agent on the planet" and shipped 160 app integrations in three weeks using its own agent to do the work. Stripe built Kai, an internal knowledge agent, in one week and reached 5,000 users in a month. LangChain shipped Managed Deep Agents with durable execution and browser use built in, publishing the seven-tool bake-off they ran before picking Stagehand instead of just shipping the first thing that worked. The pattern: infrastructure (routing, retrieval, observability, browser control) matters more than raw model capability for production agents, and companies are standardizing these pieces fast.

- **Personal agents are racing on breadth of integration, not quality of reasoning.** Instinct shipped four partnerships in one week (iMessage group chats, 1Password, Shop Pay, OpenTable) while Figma's PM team showed what "AI-native org" looks like with a 31-skill shared repo used by 60+ PMs and AutoXP, an agent that runs growth experiments autonomously with one human checkpoint before launch. Meanwhile Notion repositioned away from "AI workspace" toward "context for every agent," and Perplexity, Meta Muse, and Instinct are all converging on "goals not prompts" as the user-facing interface. The differentiation question has moved from "whose model is smartest" to "whose agent can actually touch more of the systems users already depend on" and "how do you let an agent act without requiring the user to babysit it."

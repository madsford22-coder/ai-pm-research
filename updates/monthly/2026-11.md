# November 2026 Research Summary

The shift from "AI can do tasks" to "AI can touch everything" arrived this month, and it's being arbitrated by who controls the integrations. Notion used its own agent to build 160 app connections in three weeks—a project that would've taken a partnerships team a quarter. Instinct shipped four real-world integrations (1Password, Shop Pay, OpenTable, iMessage) in five days. LangChain packaged the deep-agent runtime so teams stop building it themselves. The pattern is unmistakable: breadth of real-world reach is now the competitive moat, not model quality.

The cost structure of this shift is finally visible too. Notion rebuilt its entire AI pricing around a usage-based credit system because long-running agents—the exact kind Instinct and LangChain are betting on—make flat-rate pricing unsustainable. Figma's PM team solved the individual-vs-shared-infrastructure problem by building a 31-skill repo used by 60+ PMs instead of letting everyone freelance their own prompts. The infrastructure tax is getting paid, just in different ways.

## What Matters

- **Agent reach is now the differentiator, not reasoning quality.** Instinct expanded to iMessage group chats, Shop Pay (beyond Shopify), 1Password, and OpenTable in one week. Notion connected its agent to 160 new apps using its own agent to do the integration work. Meanwhile, businesses are actively reaching out to Instinct asking "how do we reorient our checkout/auth flows to be legible to agents," the exact inbound interest companies once had around SEO or app store optimization. This is land-grab phase—whoever's agent can actually touch more of the systems users depend on wins, partnership by partnership.

- **Shared AI infrastructure beats individual prompting at every scale.** Figma's growth team built PM OS, a standardized 31-skill repo used by 60+ PMs instead of 60+ versions of the same workflow. They paired it with AutoXP, an agent that runs growth experiments autonomously with one human checkpoint before launch—not "human in the loop at every step," just one gate placed right before external impact. This is the pattern that matters for your org: if multiple people are solving the same problem with different prompts, you have a standardization opportunity that will compound over a quarter.

- **Metered access and model routing are how you keep unit economics sane while shipping agents.** Notion flipped from flat-rate AI to a credit system paired with bring-your-own-AI (let users plug in their own model keys) and smarter model routing (right model for the task, not always the most expensive one). The routing piece is the lever—you can quietly switch a user's query to a cheaper model and they don't notice. This is the hedge every PM shipping agentic features will need eventually: credits cap your downside, BYO-AI lets power users route around your margin problem, and routing lets you defend margin without the user seeing the seams.

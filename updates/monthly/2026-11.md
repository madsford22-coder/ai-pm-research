# November 2026 Research Summary

Nobody's figured out how to make AI features profitable yet, and the entire industry is hedging simultaneously. Notion rebuilt its pricing around credits and added a bring-your-own-model escape hatch the same week Amjad Masad warned everyone that subsidized AI subscriptions are about to "rug pull," and LangChain dropped managed agent infrastructure that makes long-running agents (the thing that breaks unit economics) actually viable at scale.

The real competition this month isn't about whose model is smarter—it's about whose agent can actually touch the most real systems you depend on, and whether that breadth is sustainable without turning every account takeover into a security nightmare.

## What Matters

- **AI pricing is converging on credits + bring-your-own-model, because flat-rate pricing breaks when agents run long tasks.** Notion's new system explicitly routes queries to cheaper models when possible, letting power users bring their own API keys instead of paying Notion's markup. Amjad Masad warned the same week that subsidized AI subscriptions won't survive contact with real long-running workloads. This isn't a trend yet—it's three independent vendors hitting the same wall and solving it the same way.

- **Credential and autonomy delegation is where the design actually happens.** LangChain's browser-use handoff (don't give the agent your password, let humans authenticate mid-flow), Figma's AutoXP (one checkpoint before experiments go live), and Instinct's four partnership launches in one week (1Password, Shop Pay, OpenTable, iMessage groups) are all solving the same underlying question differently: how much can we let the agent do without a human watching every step? Figma's answer—full autonomy except one gate before external impact—is the clearest pattern so far.

- **Agents are now a productivity lever for building more agents.** Notion used its own agent to build 160 app integrations in three weeks (a task that would take a partnerships team multiple quarters), while Figma's 60+ PMs all pull from a shared 31-skill repo instead of freelancing their own prompts. The pattern is the same in both: shared infrastructure compounds faster than individual usage, and that infrastructure is now built by agents, not just used by them.

# October 2026 Research Summary

The agent wars aren't being won at the model layer anymore—they're being won in the orchestration layer around it. LangChain cut coding task costs by 64% using a model router in their harness, Notion shipped a skills library for managing agent behavior across teams, and Vistaly rebuilt its entire product around evals and repair loops to make AI-generated discovery artifacts trustworthy. The pattern is clear: once base model quality plateaus, the real differentiation is in how you route work, govern behavior, catch errors, and recover from them.

For product teams, this means the unsexy infrastructure work—routing logic, eval frameworks, shared memory systems—is becoming the actual moat. If you're still thinking of agents as "just a better prompt," you're already behind.

## What Matters

- **Evals and repair loops are the hidden cost of synthesis features.** Vistaly didn't just add AI to opportunity solution trees; they rebuilt the product around systematic ways to catch and correct bad AI output. Teresa Torres walks through how interview synthesis looks plausible but quietly misleads without eval infrastructure that specifically tests whether extracted opportunities actually reflect what customers said. Most teams shipping "AI summarizes your research" skip this step entirely, which means they're shipping unvalidated synthesis to their PMs.

- **Model routing in the harness cuts costs without cutting quality.** LangChain demonstrated a 64% median cost reduction per coding task by routing cheaper tasks to cheaper models and expensive reasoning to expensive models, all decided at the harness layer before execution. This isn't theoretical—it's production data from Open SWE's actual workload, with no measurable quality regression. If you're sending every task to your most expensive model by default, you're burning margin.

- **Team infrastructure for agents is consolidating around existing tools.** Notion shipped a skills library that lets teams author, version, and govern agent skills using the same editing and permissioning model they already use for docs and specs. Combined with their earlier shared memory system ("Lore"), Notion is betting that the artifact defining how agents behave shouldn't require a new tool—it should live where your team already works. This is the same move that turned individual AI tools into team infrastructure.

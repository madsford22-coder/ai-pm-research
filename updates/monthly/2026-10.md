# October 2026 Research Summary

The agent conversation shifted this month from "can it work?" to "how do we scale it?" Notion shipped a skills library and shared memory layer for agents, LangChain dropped a 64% cost reduction by routing tasks to cheaper models, and Linear's bug autofix loop fixed 300+ bugs in 30 days—three different signals pointing at the same thing: the bottleneck isn't capability anymore, it's governance, cost, and institutional memory.

The discovery side shows the same pattern. Vistaly rebuilt its entire product around AI interview synthesis with explicit repair loops and evals, proving that even judgment-heavy work can benefit from AI-as-accelerator if you build the right guardrails. The takeaway for PMs: stop asking whether AI can do the work, and start asking how to make it trustworthy and affordable at scale.

## What Matters

- **Team infrastructure beats raw capability.** Notion's skills library lets teams store "what good looks like" in their existing document model, then deploy those skills across any agent that plugs into Notion. Same problem Vistaly solved on the discovery side with repair loops—individual judgment doesn't scale without shared places to store decisions and keep AI honest. If your team is already using agents, the next move isn't a smarter model, it's a place to govern what agents know and remember.

- **Model routing cuts costs 64% without sacrificing quality.** LangChain built a router that picks cheaper models for simpler coding tasks and reserves expensive ones for genuinely hard work, cutting median cost per task by nearly two-thirds with no measurable quality loss. If you're treating all your AI requests as equal and routing them to the same model, you're almost certainly overpaying. Start by auditing your actual usage logs to see what fraction of requests actually need your most expensive model.

- **Evals and repair loops are how PMs keep judgment in the loop.** Vistaly didn't try to automate away PM judgment in discovery—it automated the mechanical synthesis work, then built explicit checks so hallucinated opportunities get caught before they become roadmap bets. This is a concrete answer to "how do we use AI without losing the work of thinking," and it scales better than hand-waving about "human-in-the-loop."

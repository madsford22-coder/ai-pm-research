# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-10-15

## The Short Version

Vercel shipped a full stack of agent-native infrastructure (persistent storage, backends, Python, a "Token Pass" pricing primitive) in a single day, Stripe's product team published a sharp framework for why AI monetization breaks traditional SaaS pricing logic, and Simon Willison surfaced the first real number on Anthropic's business: $16.8bn in quarterly revenue, up 50%, ahead of an IPO.

### Vercel - Ships Persistent Storage, Python GA, and "Token Pass" for Agent Workloads
**Source:** https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-drives-persistent-storage-for-agents, https://vercel.com/blog/python-on-vercel-is-now-generally-available, https://vercel.com/changelog/introducing-token-pass
**Credibility:** High (first-party changelog and blog posts, multiple shipped features with GA status)

**What happened:** Vercel dropped five related announcements on the same day: Vercel Drives (persistent storage built specifically for agents), general availability of Python on Vercel, a new "Backends" primitive supporting containers and long-running daemons, a public beta of Vercel Daemons, and "Token Pass," a new pricing/metering concept. Taken together, this is Vercel repositioning itself from "frontend deployment platform" to "infrastructure for agents that need to remember things, run in the background, and execute arbitrary code."

**Key technical details:**
- Vercel Drives: persistent storage designed for agent state, separate from ephemeral function storage
- Python on Vercel: GA, not beta, signaling Vercel wants to be a serious runtime for AI/ML workloads, not just JS/TS
- Backends: supports containers and "Daemons" (long-running background processes) alongside Python, a structural departure from Vercel's serverless-only origins
- Vercel Daemons: public beta, specifically framed around agents that need to run continuously rather than request/response
- Token Pass: a new pricing mechanism, details light in the changelog excerpt but named specifically around token-based usage
- Also shipped same day: Bring Your Own Cloud on AWS GA, Enterprise-Managed Authorization for Connect, MCP Events support in Connect (public beta)

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is Vercel admitting that serverless functions (their entire original architecture) don't fit how agents actually behave: agents need to remember state across sessions, run for hours without a request triggering them, and execute code outside JS. If you're building an agent product and evaluating where to host it, Vercel just closed the gap with AWS/GCP on the "can it run my agent" question while keeping its deploy-simplicity story. The MCP Events beta in Connect is also worth watching: it suggests Vercel wants to be the connective tissue between agents and enterprise data sources, not just a hosting layer.

**Critical questions:**
- What does Token Pass actually meter, and how does it compare to the token-based pricing everyone else (OpenAI, Anthropic) already uses? Is this a wrapper on top of model costs or a separate Vercel-level charge?
- Does GA Python support mean Vercel is now competing with Modal, Replicate, and AWS Lambda for ML inference workloads, not just app hosting?
- If agents need persistent storage and background daemons, how different is this architecturally from "just run a server," and what's left of the original serverless value prop?

**Action you could take today:** If your team hosts any agent-based feature on Vercel today, check whether your current workarounds for state persistence (external DB, Redis) could be simplified by Vercel Drives, and whether Daemons removes the need for a separate background-job service.

### Stripe - "The Alien Economics of AI Monetization"
**Source:** https://stripe.com/blog/industry
**Credibility:** High (first-party, authored by James Brown, Product Manager for Revenue at Stripe)

**What happened:** Stripe's Revenue product team published an analysis arguing AI monetization breaks the assumptions traditional SaaS pricing was built on. Usage, cost structure, and customer expectations are all shifting simultaneously, which is forcing AI companies to rethink pricing models in ways legacy per-seat SaaS pricing never had to.

**Key technical details:**
- Framed around three forces moving at once: usage patterns, underlying compute costs, and customer price expectations, versus traditional SaaS where seat count was the stable anchor
- Written from Stripe's vantage point of processing payments across a huge number of AI companies, giving them aggregate visibility into what pricing models are actually being tried
- Positioned as "alien economics," i.e. explicitly framing AI monetization as a different species of problem, not an incremental variation on subscription pricing

**Why it matters for PMs:** If you're setting pricing for any AI feature right now, per-seat pricing almost never matches the actual cost structure you're carrying (token costs scale with usage, not headcount). This piece is coming directly from the team watching real invoicing data across Stripe's AI customer base, which is a different and more grounded vantage point than most "how to price AI" thinkpieces. The framing as "alien economics" also matters: it's permission to stop trying to force AI pricing into subscription templates and treat it as a genuinely different problem.

**Critical questions:**
- Does Stripe have data on what percentage of its AI customers have already abandoned per-seat pricing in favor of usage-based or hybrid models? That number would be the real signal here.
- Is "alien economics" actually novel, or is this closer to classic marginal-cost-based pricing that cloud infrastructure companies solved a decade ago?
- How does this change the build-vs-buy calculus for billing infrastructure? Is Stripe's own usage-based billing tooling positioned as the answer to the problem they're describing?

**Action you could take today:** Pull your product's current unit economics (cost per active user driven by model inference) and compare it against your current pricing tier structure. If the ratio of your highest-usage users' costs to your lowest-usage users' costs is more than 3-5x, you likely have the same mismatch Stripe is describing.

### Simon Willison - Anthropic's Revenue Is Growing Nearly 50% Quarter-Over-Quarter Ahead of IPO
**Source:** https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/
**Credibility:** High (Simon Willison citing investor disclosure ahead of an Anthropic IPO)

**What happened:** Simon Willison flagged reporting that Anthropic told investors its quarterly revenue is now $16.8 billion, up nearly 50%, as the company prepares for an IPO. This is one of the first hard revenue numbers to become public for Anthropic outside of vague "ARR" claims typically floated in press cycles.

**Key technical details:**
- $16.8bn quarterly revenue figure, disclosed to investors (not a public filing, but investor-facing disclosure ahead of IPO)
- Nearly 50% growth, though the comparison period (QoQ vs YoY) isn't specified in the excerpt
- Comes as Anthropic moves toward an actual public listing, meaning more granular financials will likely become mandatory disclosure soon

**Why it matters for PMs:** Anthropic's revenue has mostly been discussed in vague "ARR run-rate" terms before now. A real quarterly figure, especially one tied to IPO disclosure requirements, is a much more trustworthy signal of enterprise and API demand for Claude than any benchmark score. If you're building on Claude or evaluating a multi-model strategy, sustained 50% growth at this revenue scale tells you Anthropic isn't going anywhere and has the capital runway to keep shipping (Claude Haiku 5.5, the source-control rework Simon also covered this week).

**Critical questions:**
- Is this revenue figure gross (including compute costs passed through to customers) or net? The distinction matters enormously for understanding actual business health versus pass-through API revenue.
- What's the revenue mix between API/enterprise versus Claude.ai consumer subscriptions? That changes what kind of company Anthropic actually is.
- Will IPO-driven disclosure requirements force Anthropic to be more transparent about usage and retention data that PMs could actually learn from?

**Action you could take today:** If your product depends on Claude API pricing stability, this is a mildly reassuring data point for vendor risk assessments, worth noting in any build-vs-buy or multi-model redundancy planning doc you're maintaining.

## The Thread

**The infrastructure layer for agents is consolidating fast, and everyone's racing to own the "agent needs to run for hours and remember things" problem.** Vercel's Drives/Daemons/Backends launch, AWS's "Trust in Depth" framework for agent autonomy, and LangChain's steady drumbeat of context-engineering posts (offloading, summarization, harness engineering) are all converging on the same realization: request/response and stateless functions were built for humans clicking buttons, not agents running unsupervised for 36+ hours (as OpenAI's Third Moment Research case study describes this week). The platforms that figure out persistent state, background execution, and trust boundaries first are going to own a meaningful chunk of the next wave of AI product infrastructure spend.

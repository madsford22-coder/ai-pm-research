# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-09-10

## The Short Version

OpenAI shipped two things today worth paying attention to: a production-ready voice API built for telephony and a ChatGPT Data agent that lets non-technical users build dashboards from company data. Meanwhile, Pieter Levels hit $25K/month in SaaS savings by vibe-coding his own replacements — and it's now more of a pattern than a stunt.

---

### OpenAI — GPT-Live-1 Brings Full-Duplex Voice to the API

**Source:** https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-live-1-in-the-api
**Credibility:** High (first-party announcement)

**What happened:** OpenAI launched GPT-Live-1, a new model in the API purpose-built for real-time, full-duplex voice conversations. It ships with stronger instruction following than previous voice models, support for custom voices, and explicit telephony support. This is the first OpenAI voice model framed as a telephony primitive rather than a demo or ChatGPT-only feature.

**Key capabilities:**
- Full-duplex voice: model listens and speaks simultaneously, no push-to-talk
- Custom voice support: developers can define voice characteristics, not just pick from a preset list
- Telephony integration: built to work with phone systems, not just in-browser sessions
- Stronger instruction following than previous Realtime API voice models
- Available in the API today (not a waitlist)

**Why it matters for PMs:** Voice has been in the API since the Realtime API launched, but "telephony support" is a different statement. That's OpenAI saying: build customer service bots, appointment reminders, outbound calling workflows. If you're building in fintech, healthcare, or any space where phone-based workflows are still the dominant channel, this is the first time the capabilities are explicitly designed for that context rather than shoehorned in. The custom voice capability also matters for product differentiation — your AI-powered phone experience can sound like your brand, not like every other GPT-powered product.

**Critical questions:**
- What does "telephony support" actually include? SIP integration? Twilio compatibility? The announcement is light on specifics.
- How does this compare to ElevenLabs' Conversational AI product, which has had telephony-adjacent capabilities for a while? Is this better, cheaper, or just more integrated?
- What are the latency numbers on real phone connections vs. in-browser? That's the number that determines whether this is usable for actual call center replacement.
- Does custom voice come with a content policy review process, or is it self-serve? That matters a lot for brand safety.

**Action you could take today:** If you have a voice feature on your roadmap or a phone-based workflow in your product, pull up the GPT-Live-1 docs and check whether "telephony support" includes the specific integration your stack uses. The delta between "it works" and "we have to build a custom bridge" is weeks of engineering.

### OpenAI — ChatGPT Work Gets a Data Agent

**Source:** https://openai.com/index/put-data-to-work
**Credibility:** High (first-party announcement)

**What happened:** OpenAI shipped a Data agent inside ChatGPT Work — the business tier. Users can connect company data sources, ask questions in natural language, uncover insights, and build interactive dashboards. This is framed explicitly as a tool for non-technical users, not a SQL assistant for analysts. The headline is "connect company data, build dashboards with AI."

**Key capabilities:**
- Connect company data sources directly to ChatGPT Work
- Natural language queries against connected data
- Dashboard creation from conversational prompts
- Available inside ChatGPT Work (business tier) — not a separate product

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is OpenAI directly going after the "AI for business data" category that Notion AI, Glean, and various BI platforms are all circling. The differentiation isn't the AI capability — it's the distribution. ChatGPT Work already has enterprise seat licenses, SSO, and admin controls. Adding a data layer turns it from a productivity assistant into something closer to an internal analytics tool. If you're building a product that competes in the "business intelligence for non-technical users" space, OpenAI just became a much more credible substitute. If you're a PM at a company evaluating BI tools, this is now a real question: do you buy a dedicated BI platform, or use the one that's already on everyone's desktop?

**Critical questions:**
- What data sources are supported at launch? Generic "company data" could mean anything from Google Sheets to Snowflake.
- Who can share dashboards with whom? The social/collaboration layer is what makes BI tools sticky — if dashboards are private, this is a power-user tool, not a team tool.
- How does this handle data governance? Enterprise buyers will ask about row-level security, audit logs, and whether their data trains future models.
- Is this a feature that survives contact with real enterprise data, or does it work great on clean CSVs and fall apart on messy production data?

**Action you could take today:** If you work at a company with a ChatGPT Work subscription, spend 20 minutes connecting a non-sensitive data source and trying to build a dashboard. The gap between "impressive demo" and "replaces our current tool" will tell you something real about where the product actually is.

### Pieter Levels — $25K/Month in SaaS Replaced by Vibe-Coded Tools

**Source:** https://levels.io/replaced-saas-with-vibe-coded-services (2026-09-09)
**Credibility:** Medium-High (first-person account from a tracked practitioner with history of sharing real numbers)

**What happened:** Pieter Levels published a breakdown of $25,000/month in SaaS he's replaced by vibe-coding his own versions. One specific example published the same day: he replaced a $249/month Scrapingbee plan with a $1/month scraper he coded himself. The broader post appears to aggregate several months of this pattern — replacing recurring SaaS costs with single-serving tools built on demand.

**Key patterns:**
- Individual services replaced include scraping infrastructure, monitoring tools, and likely communication/data tooling (full list at the source)
- The Scrapingbee replacement is concrete: $249/month to $1/month, built himself
- The total figure ($25K/month) suggests this isn't one or two tools — it's a systematic audit-and-replace workflow
- Levels has been documenting this pattern publicly for months, so there's a track record, not just one claim

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is a forcing function for the build-vs-buy question, and the economics are shifting fast. The traditional calculus was: build is expensive (engineering time), buy is predictable (SaaS subscription). What Levels is demonstrating is that for a certain class of tools — primarily infrastructure/utility tools with well-understood functionality — the build cost is now measured in hours, not sprints. That changes what "build vs. buy" means for your roadmap. It doesn't apply equally to everything (you shouldn't vibe-code your payment processor), but for scraping, monitoring, notification pipelines, and data transformation? The math looks different now. The PM implication: when you're reviewing your team's SaaS stack, the right question isn't just "what can we consolidate?" but "what's now cheap enough to build?"

**Critical questions:**
- What's the real ongoing cost? Vibe-coded tools still need maintenance, debugging, and someone who understands them when they break. Is $25K in SaaS costs actually free, or is it shifted to owner-maintenance?
- Which of these replacements are durable vs. brittle? Scraping infrastructure in particular tends to break when targets update their markup. Does a vibe-coded scraper hold up over 12 months?
- This pattern works for a solo operator. Does it scale to a team where nobody "owns" the vibe-coded tool?
- Is the $25K/month figure savings on tools Levels actually needed, or tools he stopped needing once he had alternatives?

**Action you could take today:** Pull up your team's SaaS bill and flag any tool under $500/month that's primarily used for data movement, scraping, monitoring, or internal automation. For each one, ask whether the functionality is specific enough that a well-prompted LLM could build a working replacement in under a day. That's your shortlist for the first vibe-code experiment.

## The Thread

**The "connect your data" move is becoming the AI platform land grab.** OpenAI's Data agent in ChatGPT Work, Notion's shared memory for agents, and AWS Bedrock's expanded knowledge base connectors are all the same move: get company data inside the platform, and the platform becomes harder to leave. What was a productivity tool becomes infrastructure. PMs evaluating AI tooling right now are implicitly choosing which platform gets to sit on top of their data — and that's a longer-term decision than it looks.

# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-11-04

## The Short Version

Notion blew up its own AI pricing model to switch to a credit system with bring-your-own-AI options, Amjad Masad is warning that subsidized AI subscriptions are about to get "rug pulled," and together they're pointing at the same unresolved question: nobody has actually found a sustainable AI pricing model yet, they're all still guessing.

## Notion - Agents, Rebuilt to Do More for Less (New Credit System)

**Source:** https://www.notion.com/blog/notion-ai-more-for-less
**Credibility:** High (first-party product announcement from Notion HQ)

**What happened:** Notion rebuilt its AI pricing and infrastructure around a new credit system, replacing whatever flat-rate or seat-based AI access existed before. The announcement, from co-founder Akshay Kothari, bundles three changes together: smarter model routing, long-running agents, and a bring-your-own-AI option, all unified under credits as the new unit of consumption. This lands the same week Notion also published a deep technical post on its "upgraded model router" (internally called Auto), which uses pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging to measure model quality at scale and decide which model handles which request.

**Key technical details:**
- New credit-based consumption model replaces previous AI access structure
- "Auto" model router uses pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging to route requests to the right model automatically
- Bring-your-own-AI option lets teams plug in their own model/API keys instead of consuming Notion's credits
- Long-running agents are now part of the core offering, not a separate SKU
- Positioned explicitly as "more for less" — i.e., a response to cost pressure, not a price increase

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is the tell that AI margins are still brutal even for a company as well-capitalized as Notion. When you see a company rebuild pricing around credits and ship a model router to automatically pick cheaper models when possible, that's a company managing inference costs as a first-class product constraint, not an afterthought. The bring-your-own-AI option is the more interesting signal: it's Notion admitting that for some customers, the economics only work if the customer eats the inference cost directly. If you're pricing AI features today, "credits" plus "optional BYO model" is becoming the default enterprise pattern, not a fringe case.

**Critical questions:**
- Does a credit system increase or decrease AI feature adoption? Credits are legible to finance teams but confusing to end users who don't think in token economics.
- What happens to users who exhausted their credits mid-task with a long-running agent? Is there graceful degradation or a hard stop?
- Is model routing + BYO-AI a genuine cost-saving architecture or a hedge against being commoditized by whichever foundation model is cheapest that month?

**Action you could take today:** Pull up your own AI feature's unit economics and ask whether you know your cost-per-active-user this month versus three months ago. If you don't know, that's the gap Notion's model router is trying to close for them.

## Amjad Masad - Subsidized AI Subscriptions Are Heading for a "Rug Pull"

**Source:** https://x.com/amasad
**Credibility:** Medium (founder/CEO opinion post, not a controlled study, but Masad runs Replit and has direct visibility into AI product economics)

**What happened:** Replit CEO Amjad Masad posted a warning this week: "If you're enjoying a subsidized AI subscription get ready to get rug pulled." He paired this with a separate post describing the current moment as "the chaotic phase of enterprise adoption of agents and vibe coding," and a third post flagging that AI agents running on employee endpoints are becoming a "systemic security risk" for enterprises and governments, citing companies like Goldman Sachs restricting tools like Claude Code.

**Key capabilities:** Not a product launch, but a founder-level read on the market: Masad is directly connecting three dots that usually get treated separately in this industry: unsustainable subsidized pricing, chaotic enterprise rollout of coding agents, and the security exposure created by letting agents run with real credentials on real endpoints.

**Why it matters for PMs:** Masad runs a company that is itself subsidizing AI coding access to drive adoption, so this is a rare moment of a vendor warning their own category about its own business model. If you're building on top of cheap inference or a subsidized third-party AI subscription, the person selling you that subsidy is telling you not to assume it lasts. For PMs planning roadmaps that depend on a model API staying cheap, or planning budgets that assume current Claude/GPT pricing is stable, this is a prompt to stress-test what your product looks like at 2x or 3x current inference costs.

**Critical questions:**
- Which parts of your product's AI functionality would you cut first if your underlying model costs doubled?
- Is "subsidized" pricing creating usage patterns (e.g., agent autonomy, longer context, more frequent calls) that won't survive a price correction?
- How does the security concern (agents with endpoint access) interact with the pricing concern? Cheaper agents probably means more deployed, unmanaged agent instances, not fewer.

**Action you could take today:** Model your AI feature's cost structure at 2x and 3x current per-token pricing and see which features break even, and which become unviable. If you haven't done this exercise, do it before your finance team asks you to.

## The Thread

**AI pricing is still a live experiment, and everyone's hedging differently.** This week alone: Notion moved to credits plus bring-your-own-model, Masad warned that subsidized subscriptions won't last, and Vercel's AI Gateway keeps adding more "ZDR" (zero data retention) model providers for enterprise buyers who don't trust the default terms. Nobody has converged on the right model yet. The pattern underneath all of it is that inference cost is now a product design constraint on par with latency or reliability, not a backend detail PMs can ignore.

## Sit With This

Notion just rebuilt its entire AI pricing model around credits and added a bring-your-own-AI option, essentially giving large customers an escape hatch from Notion's own markup on inference.

**For your product:** If a chunk of your power users would save money by bringing their own model/API key instead of paying your markup, what does that tell you about where your AI feature's actual value lives: in the model, or in everything you've built around it? Would you ship a BYO option today, or does that admission scare you more than losing those customers to a cheaper competitor?

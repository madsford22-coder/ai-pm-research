# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-10-23

## The Short Version

Notion rebuilt its AI pricing around a credit system while publishing exactly how its model router actually works, 1Password shipped a trust layer specifically for Meta's Muse agent, and Dare Obasanjo's read on Meta's earnings call numbers gives us the clearest data point yet on how fast people adopt an agent when it ships inside apps they already use.

### Notion - Rebuilt AI Pricing Around a Credit System, Then Showed Its Work on Model Quality

**Source:** https://www.notion.com/blog/notion-ai-more-for-less
**Credibility:** High (first-party product announcement, paired with a companion engineering post explaining methodology)

**What happened:** Notion rebuilt Notion AI around smarter model routing, long-running agents, bring-your-own-AI support, and a new credit-based pricing system. Akshay Kothari, Notion's co-founder, framed it as "more for less": faster responses and lower cost per task, in exchange for moving away from flat unlimited-feeling AI access toward a credit meter. Alongside the pricing post, Notion published a second piece from engineering manager Ghita Houir Alami explaining how the upgraded model router actually gets evaluated: pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging used to measure model quality at scale before routing decisions get made.

**Key technical details:**
- New credit system replaces (or supplements) previous AI access model, tied to usage rather than flat subscription inclusion
- Model router now picks between models per-task using calibrated judging, not a static default
- Bring-your-own-AI option lets teams plug in their own model/API keys instead of using Notion's default routing
- Long-running agents are now a supported product surface, not just single-turn Q&A
- Companion post details the actual evaluation methodology (pairwise comparison + calibration) used to decide which model handles which task

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is the second time in two weeks Notion has published not just what they shipped but how they decided it was good enough to ship (see also their 160-apps-in-three-weeks agent post). That's rare and worth studying: most companies say "we use the best model for the job," Notion is showing the actual pairwise-judging pipeline behind that claim. The credit-system move also answers one of our open questions directly: Notion is betting users will tolerate metered AI pricing if it's paired with visible quality and speed gains, rather than assuming AI has to feel free and unlimited to be adopted.

**Critical questions:**
- Will users perceive credits as a downgrade even if the underlying model routing genuinely improves task completion speed?
- How transparent is the credit consumption to end users in the moment, before vs. after a task runs?
- Does bring-your-own-AI cannibalize Notion's own credit revenue from power users who'll just plug in their own API key?

**Action you could take today:** If your product has flat-rate AI access, pull your cost-per-task data and model it against a credit system like Notion's, specifically where your heaviest users sit relative to a hypothetical credit cap.

### 1Password - Shipped a Trust Layer Built Specifically for Meta's Muse Agent

**Source:** https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/1password-agents-launches-trust-layer-130000662.html
**Credibility:** High (product launch with on-record quote from Nat Friedman, Head of Product at Meta Superintelligence Labs)

**What happened:** 1Password launched "1Password for Agents," integrating directly into Meta's Muse so the agent can access services on a user's behalf without the user handing over raw credentials. Nat Friedman gave the quote that frames the whole thing: "Muse is like an AI personal assistant and 1Password gives it safe access to services, at your direction." This is a credential-delegation product built specifically for the personal-agent category, not a generic password manager feature.

**Key capabilities:**
- Agents authenticate to third-party services through 1Password rather than being handed plaintext passwords
- Access is scoped "at your direction," implying per-action or per-session permissioning rather than blanket credential handoff
- Built in direct partnership with Meta, positioning 1Password as infrastructure for the personal-agent category broadly, not just Muse

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is the missing piece in the "how much autonomy will users give an agent" question. The answer so far has been "not much, because credential sharing feels unsafe." A dedicated trust layer from an established password manager is the kind of infrastructure that could unlock the next tier of agent autonomy, the same way OAuth unlocked third-party app ecosystems once nobody had to type their password into a stranger's app anymore. Watch whether Instinct or other personal-agent competitors adopt something similar or build in-house.

**Critical questions:**
- What happens when the agent needs to act on a site 1Password hasn't integrated with yet, does it fall back to asking the user for raw credentials?
- Is there an audit trail users can review after the fact to see exactly what the agent did with delegated access?
- Does this become a chokepoint 1Password can monetize independently of Meta, or is it effectively Muse-exclusive infrastructure?

**Action you could take today:** If your product has any agent or automation feature that touches third-party accounts, check whether you're asking users for raw credentials anywhere, and flag it as a trust-layer gap worth fixing before a competitor ships one.

### Dare Obasanjo - Meta's Earnings Call Reveals Muse Adoption Numbers and a 10x Cost Efficiency Bet

**Source:** https://www.threads.com/@carnage4life
**Credibility:** Medium (secondhand commentary on an earnings call, but from a credible enterprise-software voice summarizing primary company disclosures)

**What happened:** Dare Obasanjo posted a breakdown of Meta's earnings commentary on Muse: more than 10 million people set up Muse in the US and Canada in roughly six weeks, with about 25% daily active use, and shopping as the most popular use case. Meta also said it expects more than 10x improvement in Muse's compute cost efficiency next year through smaller models and GPU optimization.

**Why it matters for PMs:** Two separate signals here worth separating. First, the adoption number: 10M setups in six weeks with 25% DAU is a real, usable data point against the open question of whether AI agents drive engagement beyond novelty, and it directly contradicts the "agents are a chatbot gimmick" skepticism. Second, the 10x cost efficiency claim is the more interesting one for anyone doing margin math on agent products: Meta is explicitly saying today's Muse compute cost is not the long-term cost, which changes how you'd model unit economics for any agent feature you're pricing now. If shopping is the top use case, that's also a direct signal for e-commerce and fintech PMs about what consumers actually want an agent to do first, not research or writing, but transactions.

**Critical questions:**
- Is 25% DAU good or bad relative to other "set up once" consumer features, what's the comparable baseline?
- Does "shopping as most popular use case" mean browsing/comparison, or completed transactions, those are very different trust bars?
- How much of the 10x cost efficiency projection is model improvements vs. just Meta's scale advantage in GPU procurement that smaller companies can't replicate?

**Action you could take today:** If you're pricing an agent feature based on today's inference costs, build a second model assuming a 10x cost reduction in 12 months and see whether your pricing strategy changes.

## The Thread

**Trust and credential delegation is becoming the real bottleneck for agent autonomy, not model capability.** This week alone: 1Password built a dedicated trust layer for Muse, Vercel published how its own Agent accesses production safely, and LangSmith shipped runtime controls (spend caps, rate limits, PII redaction) for production agents. The industry has stopped debating whether agents can do tasks and started building the permission infrastructure to let them do tasks safely. That's the real product unlock to watch, more than any new model release.

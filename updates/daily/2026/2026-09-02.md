### Teresa Torres — AI Evals: A Hands-On Guide for Product Teams

**Source:** https://www.producttalk.org/ai-evals/ and https://www.producttalk.org/ai-evals-test-harness/
**Credibility:** High (first-party writing from Teresa Torres, creator of Continuous Discovery Habits framework, published alongside a downloadable test harness)

**What happened:** Teresa Torres published two pieces today: a full conceptual guide to AI evals for product teams, and a companion post with the actual test harness tool she uses to run them. The guide covers error analysis, four types of evals, and how to run your first experiment. The test harness post is the practical layer — it explains the "baseline problem" (you have to run every input through your current system before you can measure improvement) and gives you the tool to do that.

**Key patterns:**
- Four types of evals (not specified in the excerpt, but the framing is error analysis first, then systematic measurement)
- Baseline problem: you can't evaluate improvement without first capturing your current system's outputs — which requires running every test case through the existing system before making changes
- The test harness is a reusable tool, not a one-time exercise — the framing is "your first experiment," implying this becomes infrastructure

**Why it matters for PMs:** Most PMs building AI features have no idea if the thing is getting better or worse between model upgrades. Evals are the gap between "we think this improved" and "we know it improved." Teresa's framework is grounded in product team workflows, not ML engineering — it's designed for the people making product decisions, not the people training models. The test harness in particular removes the activation energy barrier. If your team doesn't have evals, this is the most concrete starting point I've seen written for a PM audience.

**Critical questions:**
- How do you define what counts as a "correct" output for open-ended AI tasks? The guide presumably addresses this, but the answer shapes everything downstream.
- At what scale do evals become necessary? A team shipping an AI writing feature to 100 users might not need the same rigor as one shipping to 100,000.
- Who owns evals on a product team — PM, engineering, or a dedicated AI quality function? This is still an open org design question.
- How do you handle evals when the right answer changes over time (e.g., tone guidelines evolve, user expectations shift)?

**Action you could take today:** Go grab the test harness at producttalk.org/ai-evals-test-harness and run it against one AI feature your team owns. Even if you don't use the full framework, establishing a baseline output before your next model upgrade is worth doing right now.

### Anthropic — Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news (announcement summary) and https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-claude-fable-5-1-on-aws/
**Credibility:** High (first-party announcements from Anthropic and AWS)

**What happened:** Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on September 1. Fable 5.1 is positioned as their most advanced model for coding and knowledge work, with research capabilities that offer an early glimpse of future directions. The AWS post confirms it's available on Amazon Bedrock with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards — meaning enterprise customers get data residency controls in their cloud environment. Dan Shipper at Every called it "the strongest coding model we've used, but now it's fast." Simon Willison confirmed the animated pelican demo worked well (a quick creative generation test he uses as a benchmark). The model also shipped with a new system prompt that's notably aggressive about not reproducing song lyrics — Willison flagged this as worth watching.

**Key technical details:**
- Available on Amazon Bedrock immediately at launch, with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards for data control
- Positioned as fast AND strong — prior Fable versions were noted for quality but criticized for speed
- Also available through Vercel's AI Gateway (confirmed in Vercel changelog)
- New system prompt behavior around copyright-sensitive content (song lyrics specifically) — a policy change, not just a model update
- Research capabilities described as "an early glimpse" — suggesting agentic research workflows are a roadmap signal

**Why it matters for PMs:** Every time a new frontier model drops, the question PMs should ask isn't "is it better" — it's "does this change the build calculus?" Fable 5.1's speed improvement matters a lot for user-facing features where latency is a product experience issue. The fact that it's shipping simultaneously on Bedrock with enterprise data controls tells you Anthropic is moving aggressively on enterprise sales. If your team is currently on an older Claude version for a production feature, this is a prompt to re-evaluate. The copyright system prompt change is a different kind of signal — Anthropic is tightening policy through the model itself, not just through terms of service. That's a product decision worth understanding if you're building anything that involves content generation.

**Critical questions:**
- How much faster is "now it's fast"? Speed claims without latency numbers are hard to act on.
- The "Enterprise Frontier Safeguards" on Bedrock — are these Anthropic-native controls or AWS-layer controls? The answer matters for who you negotiate compliance with.
- The copyright system prompt: is this a response to legal pressure, or proactive safety posture? The motivation affects whether it'll tighten further.
- Does Mythos 5.1 (the other model in this release) serve a different use case than Fable? The announcement bundles them but doesn't explain the distinction clearly.

**Action you could take today:** If your team uses Claude in any production feature, check the Vercel AI Gateway changelog and the AWS Bedrock availability notice to see if Fable 5.1 is available in your stack. Run your existing eval set (or if you don't have one, go read Teresa Torres's guide above) against the new model before switching.

### Perplexity — Hybrid Compute for Mac App

**Source:** https://x.com/AravSrinivas (posted September 2, 2026)
**Credibility:** High (direct from Aravind Srinivas, Perplexity CEO)

**What happened:** Perplexity launched hybrid compute for all users of the Perplexity Mac app. The feature lets Perplexity Computer orchestrate local models running on-device (on Mac) for agent steps that involve sensitive or private files, while using cloud compute for everything else. This is a meaningful architectural move: the privacy argument for local processing is built directly into the product experience. Perplexity also announced Coinbase integration for Computer — crypto market analysis and trading execution through an agent, with 20+ financial connectors and no MCP setup required.

**Key technical details:**
- Local model orchestration for private file analysis — cloud handles the rest
- Portable Computer also demoed on NVIDIA DGX Spark on September 2 — showing the local compute story extends beyond just Mac
- 20+ financial connectors in Perplexity Computer, including Coinbase — no API setup, just "click connect"
- This is Mac-only for now (no mention of Windows or iOS)

**Why it matters for PMs:** Hybrid compute is the answer to the privacy objection in enterprise and prosumer AI tool adoption. The pattern here: instead of asking users to trust that their data is secure in the cloud, Perplexity is letting sensitive data never leave the device. That's a different trust architecture than "we have SOC 2 compliance." For PMs building tools where users handle sensitive data (finance, health, legal), this is the pattern to watch — not just "we're secure" but "your data doesn't move." The Coinbase integration is a separate but interesting signal: Perplexity is positioning Computer as an agent that actually *does things* in financial contexts, not just researches them. That's a much higher-stakes autonomy bet than most AI tools have made.

**Critical questions:**
- What local models are running on Mac? Apple Silicon has real constraints on what models are viable locally — this matters for quality expectations.
- "No MCP setup" for financial connectors sounds great, but what's the auth and security model for connecting a trading account to an AI agent?
- Is this hybrid compute architecture available to developers building on Perplexity, or only in the first-party app?
- The Coinbase integration raises serious questions about error recovery: if an agent executes a trade incorrectly, what's the user's recourse?

**Action you could take today:** If you're building a product where users handle sensitive documents or data, sketch out what a hybrid compute architecture would look like for your use case — what stays local, what goes cloud, and how you'd explain that boundary to users in plain language.

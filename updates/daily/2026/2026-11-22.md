# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-11-22

## The Short Version

Notion just told the world its next chapter isn't about humans using Notion, it's about being "the best context system for every agent on the planet," Stripe shipped a Treasury feature that lets AI agents move money with controlled permissions, and Noah Shinn dropped a number worth sitting with: people who've made a purchase through Instinct are spending over $700/month through the agent.

### Notion - "The Next Era of Notion": From Workspace to Agent Context Layer
**Source:** https://www.notion.com/blog/the-next-era-of-notion
**Credibility:** High (first-party strategic announcement from Ivan Zhao, co-founder/CEO)

**What happened:** Ivan Zhao published a strategic repositioning of Notion: "Our next chapter is Notion as the best context system for every agent on the planet, helping teams and agents work together." This isn't a feature launch, it's Notion declaring what it thinks it's actually for going forward. The post arrives alongside a cluster of related ships: an upgraded model router (Auto) built by engineering manager Ghita Houir Alami that uses pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging to measure model quality at scale, a rebuilt AI system ("Agents, rebuilt to do more for less") from co-founder Akshay Kothari with smarter routing, long-running agents, bring-your-own-AI options, and a new credit system, and a piece from Eileen Jiang on how a Notion agent connected Notion AI to 160 new third-party apps in three weeks.

**Key technical details:**
- Auto model router uses pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging, not single-score benchmarks, to decide which model handles which task
- New credit-based pricing system replaces (or sits alongside) previous AI usage structure, explicitly framed as "more for less"
- Bring-your-own-AI option lets teams plug in their own model/API keys rather than being locked to Notion's routing
- Long-running agents are now a first-class capability, not just single-turn Q&A
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the connective tissue: five customer stories published showing teams connecting Notion to Claude, ChatGPT, and other agents specifically to keep "work where the team can find it and context where agents can use it"
- The 160-app integration in three weeks was done by having a Notion agent handle the Figma setup, connection, and testing process itself, not a human integration team

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is the clearest "pick a side" move we've seen from a tracked company this quarter. Notion isn't adding AI features to a human-first product anymore, it's restructuring the product so agents are a primary user alongside humans. If you're building anything that stores institutional knowledge (docs, wikis, project trackers), the question Notion is forcing is: are you optimizing your data model for human readability or agent retrievability? Those aren't always the same design, and Notion just bet its roadmap on the second one. The bring-your-own-AI option is also worth watching closely, it suggests Notion doesn't think it can win by locking you into its own model, only by owning the context layer underneath whichever model you use.

**Critical questions:**
- If Notion becomes the "context system for every agent," does that make the product more sticky (more integrations, more lock-in) or more commoditized (just a context store any agent can read)?
- What happens to Notion's human-facing UX quality as it optimizes increasingly for agent legibility? Are those genuinely aligned incentives or a tradeoff Notion is underselling?
- The credit system change is described as "more for less," but what's the actual unit economics shift? Pricing changes framed as savings are worth checking against prior tier structures before taking the framing at face value.

**Action you could take today:** If your product has a data layer that other tools or agents might need to read (project data, customer records, docs), audit whether that data is structured for a human UI or for programmatic/agent access, and whether MCP or a similar protocol is on your roadmap at all.

### Stripe - Treasury Now Lets Agents Analyze Data and Move Funds With Controlled Access
**Source:** https://stripe.com/blog/product
**Credibility:** High (first-party product announcement, authored by Meredith Neyrand, PM for Stripe Treasury)

**What happened:** Stripe shipped a feature in Stripe Treasury that gives AI agents controlled, scoped access to analyze financial data and actually move funds, not just read-only reporting. Published November 19 and authored directly by the Treasury PM, this is Stripe answering the "how much autonomy should an agent have over money" question with a real permissions model rather than a blog post about principles.

**Key capabilities:**
- Agents get scoped access rather than full account control, the "controlled access" framing suggests permission boundaries (what the agent can see, what it can move, likely transaction limits)
- Positioned specifically around analysis + action together, agents can both query financial data and execute fund movements based on what they find
- Built into Stripe Treasury, which underlies a lot of embedded finance and neobank infrastructure, so this isn't a toy demo, it's infrastructure other fintech products will build on top of

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is the single most concrete data point this week on the open question of agent autonomy in financial products. Every PM building fintech or embedded finance features has been asking "how do you let an agent act on money without creating a liability nightmare," and Stripe just shipped an answer: scoped, permissioned access rather than all-or-nothing. If you're building anything where an agent touches money movement, even indirectly (expense approvals, subscription management, transfers), this is the pattern to study before you invent your own.

**Critical questions:**
- What's the actual permission granularity? Per-transaction limits, velocity limits, allowlisted recipients? The announcement doesn't specify, and that detail is the whole ballgame for risk teams evaluating this.
- Who's liable when a scoped agent does something scoped-but-wrong, like moving funds to a valid but unintended recipient? Scoping reduces blast radius, it doesn't eliminate the error class.
- Is this positioned for Stripe's own agent products or for third-party developers building agents on Treasury? The framing suggests the latter, which is a bigger platform bet.

**Action you could take today:** If your product has any agent-adjacent money movement roadmap item, pull up Stripe's permission model as a reference architecture before your next design review, specifically the scoping mechanism, not just the headline capability.

### Noah Shinn / Instinct - Personal Agent Users Spend $700+/Month Once They Make a First Purchase
**Source:** https://x.com/noahrshinn (Nov 18, 2026 post)
**Credibility:** Medium (founder's own claim, no independent verification, but directly relevant primary-source data point)

**What happened:** Noah Shinn, founder of Instinct, posted that people who've made at least one purchase through the personal AI agent have an average spend of over $700/month through the product. This is the first concrete usage/spend number we've seen from Instinct since it launched, and it directly addresses one of the open questions this digest has been tracking: how much autonomy, and how much money, will people actually hand to a personal agent.

**Key capabilities:**
- The stat is specifically about users who've converted past first purchase, not average across all users, this is a retention/expansion signal among already-activated users, not an acquisition metric
- Comes the same week Instinct's related company (Applied Compute, per Shinn's other post) was acquired by Palo Alto Networks for $4.5B, suggesting real financial momentum in the adjacent ecosystem

**Why it matters for PMs:** $700/month in agent-mediated spend, if accurate, is a genuinely surprising retention number for a category that's less than a year into mainstream existence. It suggests that once someone trusts a personal agent enough to buy something through it once, they don't treat it as a novelty, they start routing meaningful recurring spend through it. That's the opposite of what skeptics of agent commerce have assumed (that people would use agents for small, low-trust purchases only). If true at scale, this reshapes how fintech and commerce PMs should think about agent checkout flows, this isn't incremental convenience, it's a new high-intent spend channel.

**Critical questions:**
- Is this self-reported by the founder with no sample size, cohort definition, or methodology disclosed? Founder-stated metrics without context (N=?, over what time period, what counts as "a purchase") deserve real skepticism before you build a strategy around them.
- Is $700/month driven by a small number of power users (travel bookings, big one-off purchases) or broad-based recurring spend? Those have very different product implications.
- How does this compare to non-agent-mediated spend for the same users? Is the agent driving incremental spend or just redirecting existing spend through a new channel?

**Action you could take today:** If you're tracking the agentic commerce open question, add this data point to your working file but flag it as unverified, then watch for Instinct or a third party to publish methodology before treating it as evidence rather than anecdote.

## The Thread

**Every tracked company this week is answering the same question differently: who is the user, human or agent?** Notion explicitly restructured its roadmap around agents as primary users. Stripe built scoped permissions assuming agents will move money, not just read data. Figma put an agent directly into Dev Mode as a design collaborator. LangChain built infrastructure to keep agents' knowledge current without human intervention. The common thread isn't "AI features," it's products quietly redesigning their core data models and permission systems around non-human users. The PM implication: if your product's information architecture was designed purely for human UX, you may be behind on a shift that's already showing up in four separate tracked companies' shipped work this week.

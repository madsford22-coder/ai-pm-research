### Julie Zhuo — You Cannot Mandate an AI Transformation

**Source:** https://joulee.medium.com/you-cannot-mandate-an-ai-transformation-e2513cc3bbcc
**Credibility:** High (first-party post from a respected product leader and former VP of Design at Facebook)

**What happened:** Julie Zhuo published a piece arguing that AI transformation cannot be driven by top-down mandate. The core argument: when organizations try to force AI adoption through policy, requirements, or mandates, they get compliance theater — people using AI tools performatively without changing how they actually work. Real transformation happens when people experience genuine value and pull AI into their workflows themselves.

**Key patterns:**
- Mandated adoption produces surface-level usage, not behavioral change — people will check the box without changing the underlying work
- The unit of transformation is individual value, not organizational policy: someone has to personally experience a moment where AI made their work meaningfully better
- PMs and leaders accelerate adoption by creating conditions for those individual "aha" moments — not by setting quotas or requirements
- The temptation to mandate is highest when adoption is slow, but that's exactly when mandating backfires — it signals the tool isn't generating enough pull on its own

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is directly relevant to anyone trying to drive internal AI adoption on a product team or selling an AI product to enterprise buyers. The pattern Zhuo is describing — compliance theater vs. genuine adoption — maps directly to the retention problem with AI features. If users feel like they're "supposed to" use the feature rather than wanting to, you won't see real engagement data and you'll misread the signals. It also challenges the instinct to solve slow adoption with more visibility, more reminders, or more requirements. The diagnosis is usually a value problem, not a awareness problem.

**Critical questions:**
- How do you distinguish genuine pull-based adoption from compliance in your usage metrics? What would each look like?
- If the unit of transformation is individual value, what does your onboarding do to reliably create that first "aha" moment?
- For B2B AI products: are your champions using the product because it helps them, or because they bought it and need to justify the purchase?
- At what point does a mandate become appropriate? Are there cases where forcing early exposure is actually the right call to generate enough usage for people to discover value?

**Action you could take today:** Look at your AI feature's activation funnel. Find the step where users drop off before reaching the core value moment and ask honestly: are the people who get past that point using it because they want to, or because they set it up and feel obligated? The answer changes your next experiment.

### OpenAI — ChatGPT Connects to EHR and Healthcare Data Sources

**Source:** https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-connects-health-records-and-healthcare-sources
**Credibility:** High (first-party announcement from OpenAI)

**What happened:** OpenAI launched the ability for healthcare organizations to connect electronic health record (EHR) systems and additional trusted healthcare data sources directly to ChatGPT. The use case is clinician-facing: doctors and clinical staff can access patient context, medical research, and other healthcare data inside a ChatGPT interface without leaving their workflow. This is a significant vertical expansion — ChatGPT is no longer just a general-purpose assistant in healthcare settings, it's attempting to become a clinical data access layer.

**Key capabilities:**
- EHR integration: clinicians can pull patient context into ChatGPT conversations directly
- Additional trusted healthcare data sources can be connected alongside EHR data
- Positioned as a secure, clinician-facing tool — not a consumer product
- Part of a pattern of OpenAI pursuing vertical-specific enterprise configurations of ChatGPT

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is one of the clearest signals yet that OpenAI is building vertical-specific product configurations rather than waiting for enterprises to figure out integration themselves. For PMs in regulated industries (fintech, healthcare, legal), this changes the build-vs-buy calculus: you're not just buying a general model API anymore, you're potentially buying a pre-configured, compliance-aware vertical product. The question is whether OpenAI can make the security and data handling story credible enough for enterprise healthcare buyers — which is a very different bar than consumer or even standard enterprise.

**Critical questions:**
- What are the actual data handling and HIPAA compliance guarantees? The announcement describes it as "secure" but clinician trust depends on specifics.
- Is this a connector architecture (data stays in EHR systems, retrieved at query time) or does OpenAI store patient data? That distinction matters enormously.
- Who is the actual buyer — individual health systems, hospital IT departments, EHR vendors? The go-to-market path is completely different for each.
- Does this accelerate or complicate the decision for fintech/healthcare PMs building their own AI features? If OpenAI is offering a pre-built vertical layer, the bar for custom builds just got higher.

**Action you could take today:** If you're building AI features in any regulated industry, pull up this announcement and map it against your current build-vs-buy assumptions. The specific capabilities OpenAI is offering (EHR connectors, trusted data source integration, secure access) are things teams typically spend months building. At minimum it's worth a conversation with your security and compliance team about what "secure" actually means in OpenAI's architecture here.

### Figma — Generative Plugins and Shaders Update

**Source:** https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/41147702210071
**Credibility:** High (official Figma changelog)

**What happened:** Figma shipped updates to generative plugins and shaders, following their Config launch of custom generative plugin support. The changelog signals that teams can now build their own custom generative AI tools inside Figma — not just use Figma's built-in AI features, but create plugin-level AI capabilities specific to their design workflows and brand systems. This is a platform move: Figma is opening the AI layer to third-party and internal builders.

**Key capabilities:**
- Custom generative plugin support: teams can build their own AI-powered plugins inside Figma
- Shader support expanded alongside generative capabilities
- Builds on Config announcements — this is the follow-through shipping after the launch event
- Extends Figma's AI from a set of Figma-owned features to a platform where teams can define their own

**Why it matters for PMs:** The shift from "AI features in Figma" to "AI platform inside Figma" is meaningful. Previously, teams using Figma AI were consuming whatever Figma built. Now design systems teams can encode brand-specific AI behaviors into plugins — custom generation rules, style enforcement, component suggestions that know your design system. This is directly relevant if you have a design systems team: the question is no longer "which Figma AI feature should we use?" but "should we build a plugin that encodes our design intelligence?" For PMs at product-led companies, this is also a template for how to extend AI capabilities through a plugin/extension layer rather than baking everything into the core product.

**Critical questions:**
- What's the actual development surface for these generative plugins — is it accessible to design engineers, or does it require specialized AI/ML work?
- How do brand and IP considerations work when a company's custom generative plugin is trained on or configured with proprietary design assets?
- Does this canibalize the market for third-party Figma plugins that do generative AI, or does it grow the pie?
- For PMs building design tools or creative software: does Figma's platform move set an expectation that your product also needs to be extensible at the AI layer?

**Action you could take today:** If you have a design systems team, forward this changelog to them with a specific question: is there one repetitive, judgment-dependent task in your design review or component workflow that a custom generative plugin could take a first pass at? That's the right scoping question for evaluating whether to build.

### AWS / Jamf — Real-Time Per-User Spend Enforcement for Amazon Bedrock

**Source:** https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/tokenomics-at-scale-how-jamf-built-real-time-spend-enforcement-for-amazon-bedrock/
**Credibility:** High (first-party AWS case study with technical implementation details)

**What happened:** Jamf published a detailed case study on how they built real-time, per-user spend enforcement for Amazon Bedrock as their AI usage scaled. The system uses IAM Customer Managed Policies, an Amazon Athena cost view, and a serverless AWS Lambda loop that applies tiered model limits in near-real-time. The problem they were solving: as generative AI adoption spread across Jamf internally, cost governance became the blocker — they needed to enforce spending limits at the individual user level without building a complex billing system from scratch.

**Key technical details:**
- IAM Customer Managed Policies used to define and enforce model access tiers
- Amazon Athena cost view provides the spend visibility layer — querying usage data in near-real-time
- AWS Lambda loop polls Athena data and dynamically updates IAM policies based on spend thresholds
- Tiered model limits: different users or roles get different spending caps, enforced automatically
- "Tokenomics" framing: treating AI token spend as a budgeting and governance problem, not just a cost center

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is one of the most practical internal AI governance case studies I've seen published. The pattern — tiered per-user budgets with automated enforcement — is exactly what PMs building internal AI tools need to think about before they go to production at scale. The "tokenomics" framing is useful: it reframes AI cost governance as a product design problem (how do you allocate a scarce resource across users?) rather than just an infrastructure problem. The Jamf architecture is also a template that teams using Bedrock can adapt directly.

**Critical questions:**
- How much engineering investment did this require, and at what scale does it become worth building versus just setting org-level spending caps?
- Does the near-real-time enforcement have meaningful latency? If there's a multi-minute lag between spend and enforcement, users can still blow through limits before the system catches up.
- How do you set tiered limits in a way that doesn't create perverse incentives (e.g., power users gaming the system by switching accounts)?
- For PMs shipping AI features externally: does this architecture translate to per-customer or per-seat spend enforcement, or is it only useful for internal tooling?

**Action you could take today:** If your team is running any AI workloads on Bedrock without per-user cost visibility, read the Jamf architecture and estimate what a minimal version of this would take to build. Even if you don't implement it immediately, knowing the cost-per-user breakdown is the first step to making informed decisions about model selection, tier design, and pricing.

# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-10-06

## The Short Version

Figma's AI agent just exited beta with real GA polish (latency, asset search, multi-file search), Mistral shipped Large 4 as the first proof point of its €3B war chest, and Karri Saarinen publicly defected from Meta's Muse to OpenAI's Dot for personal use while keeping Linear's AI for work, which is the kind of quiet, specific signal that tells you more about agent trust than any benchmark.

---

### Figma - The Figma Agent Is Now Generally Available
**Source:** https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/43509372175511-Add-guidelines-files-to-a-design-library
**Credibility:** High (first-party release notes, specific GA claim with named improvements)

**What happened:** Figma's AI agent, which had been in beta, is now generally available as of October 6. The GA release comes with improved latency and the ability to search for Figma files, moving it from an experimental add-on to a core part of the product. This lands the same week Figma also shipped multi-org GitHub connections (Oct 5) and Community "riffs" for sharing prototypes and animations (Sept 30).

**Key technical details:**
- Agent latency improved as part of the GA transition (specific numbers not published in release notes)
- New capability: the agent can now search for Figma files directly, not just operate within an open file
- Shipped alongside multi-GitHub-org support, letting teams connect more than one GitHub org to a single Figma plan
- Part of a broader cluster of releases this week: Motion got custom styles, audio, text animations, and Lottie export; mobile app got smoother collaboration and navigation

**Why it matters for PMs:** Beta-to-GA is a real decision point, not a formality, and Figma choosing to tie this one to latency and search (not new flashy capabilities) tells you where the actual adoption blocker was. If you've shipped an AI feature in beta, the lesson here is that "make it faster and make it findable" is often the unsexy work that turns a beta into something teams actually rely on daily. This also matters for anyone building AI into collaborative tools: Figma is treating the agent as infrastructure (tied to GitHub, file search, multiplayer) rather than a bolted-on chat window, which is the same pattern we've seen from Notion's skills library and shared agent memory work.

**Critical questions:**
- What was the actual beta-to-GA bar? Latency and search are measurable, but what quality threshold triggered the graduation?
- Does GA status change pricing or access tier, or is this purely a confidence signal to existing users?
- How does file search work across permissions? Can the agent surface files a user technically has access to but shouldn't be reminded exist?

**Action you could take today:** If you have an AI feature stuck in beta, audit whether the blocker is capability or infrastructure (latency, discoverability, integration depth). Figma's GA criteria suggest infrastructure fixes often matter more than new features for graduating a beta.

### Mistral AI - Mistral Large 4 Ships as First Proof Point of €3B Series D
**Source:** https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/
**Credibility:** High (first-party product announcement from CEO Arthur Mensch)

**What happened:** Mistral released Large 4, explicitly framed as "the first milestone on the roadmap funded by Mistral's €3 billion Series D." The same day, Mistral also announced a new Munich hub to "advance industrial AI in Germany," with CEO Arthur Mensch personally delivering both announcements. Vercel added Large 4 to its AI Gateway on the same day, suggesting fast multi-platform availability.

**Key technical details:**
- Positioned explicitly as a funded roadmap milestone, not a standalone release, tying model quality to capital deployment strategy
- Simultaneous European infrastructure expansion (Munich hub) alongside the model release, reinforcing the "sovereign AI" positioning
- Immediate availability via Vercel AI Gateway on release day

**Why it matters for PMs:** Mistral keeps running a different playbook than the US labs: pair every model release with a European infrastructure or market story, not just a benchmark chart. For PMs evaluating model providers, this is worth tracking as a genuine strategic differentiator, not just marketing. Arthur Mensch is building toward enterprise and government buyers who care about where compute lives and who controls it, which is a different sales motion than OpenAI or Anthropic's developer-first approach. If your buyers (especially in regulated or EU markets) are starting to ask about model sovereignty, Mistral is the company actively building for that question.

**Critical questions:**
- Does Large 4 close the capability gap with Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 Sol, or is this mainly a strategic/sovereignty play rather than a frontier model claim?
- How much of the Munich hub announcement is substance (real compute, real hires) versus messaging timed to the model launch?
- Will enterprise buyers actually switch providers for data residency reasons, or is this a hedge they want available but won't exercise?

**Action you could take today:** If you're evaluating model providers for an EU-facing product, check whether Mistral Large 4's pricing and latency on Vercel's AI Gateway are competitive enough to justify the sovereignty story, not just assume it is.

### Karri Saarinen - Linear's CEO Switches Personal AI Agent from Muse to Dot
**Source:** https://x.com/karrisaarinen/status/2107185534359761051
**Credibility:** Medium (single tweet from a tracked, credible product leader, not a formal case study)

**What happened:** Karri Saarinen, CEO of Linear, posted that he's moving from Meta's Muse to OpenAI's Dot for personal use, using ChatGPT/Codex daily for personal projects, while keeping his work AI usage centered on Linear itself because of its contextual capabilities. This is a specific, named defection from one tracked personal-agent product to another, by someone who builds product software for a living.

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is exactly the kind of signal the open questions file asks us to watch for: how much trust and switching behavior are forming around personal AI agents. Saarinen isn't a random user, he's a CEO who has spent this year publicly comparing Opus 5.5 against GPT-6 Sol and reflecting on his own AI usage patterns (he wrote about taking the summer off from AI to reassess). His choice to split allegiance, personal life to Dot, work context to Linear's own AI, suggests personal agents are being evaluated on task fit rather than brand loyalty. If users are willing to run multiple agents for different contexts rather than consolidating around one "operating system for life," that changes how Meta, OpenAI, and Instinct should think about lock-in.

**Critical questions:**
- Is this feedback specific to Muse's UX (echoing Ben Tossell's "my dot experience has been 💩" critique, interestingly reversed) or a deeper trust/capability issue with Meta's agent?
- Does one high-visibility builder switching mean anything at scale, or is this exactly the kind of anecdote that gets over-indexed because it's easy to quote?
- What does "work AI use stays with Linear" actually mean for Linear's own AI roadmap? Is Linear building toward being its own agent rather than integrating with Dot or Muse?

**Action you could take today:** If you're tracking personal-agent competitive dynamics, start logging named defections (not just usage stats) between Dot, Muse, and Instinct from credible builders. These anecdotes are early, noisy signal, but they're the only visibility we have into actual switching behavior right now.

## The Thread

**Personal agents are entering their "which one do I actually trust" phase.** This week alone we have Ben Tossell publicly panning Dot's UX, Karri Saarinen quietly switching from Muse to Dot for personal use while keeping work AI separate, and Instinct shipping group chat as a trust-building, multiplayer feature. None of these are benchmark stories. They're all about where real humans put their actual daily trust, and the answer so far is: fragmented, context-dependent, and shifting fast based on specific bad or good experiences rather than brand loyalty.

## Sit With This

Karri Saarinen is keeping his work AI usage inside Linear rather than routing it through Dot or Muse, even as he's happy to use OpenAI's agent for personal projects. That's a builder choosing context and integration depth over a general-purpose agent, even one from a company he clearly respects enough to adopt personally.

**For your product:** If you're building or evaluating an AI feature that competes with a general-purpose agent (Dot, Muse, Instinct) for the same task, what specific contextual advantage does your product have that a general agent can't replicate?

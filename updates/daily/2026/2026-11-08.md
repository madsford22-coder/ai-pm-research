# Daily PM Research Update: 2026-11-08

## The Short Version

Perplexity shipped internal numbers showing employees are getting 7x more agent-hours per week since September, Notion keeps rebuilding its AI stack around a credit system and model router instead of flat seats, and Amjad Masad's origin story for vibe coding is a reminder that the biggest product insights sometimes come from just watching what users paste into a text box.

## Perplexity - Teammates Launches, and Aravind Srinivas Shares the Adoption Numbers

**Source:** https://x.com/AravSrinivas (Nov 5-6, 2026)
**Credibility:** Medium (founder tweets with specific internal metrics, not an independently audited report, but concrete and dated)

**What happened:** Perplexity launched Teammates, a set of work-focused AI agents, on November 5. Within a day, CEO Aravind Srinivas followed up with two separate data points: the Agent API's benchmark score climbed from 55% to 77% while cost dropped nearly 90% month over month, and internally, "the median Perplexity employee now gets 7x more agent-hours per week from teammates than in September." On November 8 he also announced a partnership with Intel to bring local models and hybrid inference to Intel Ultra Series 3 laptops.

**Key technical details:**
- Teammates: work-focused AI agents built on top of Perplexity's existing agent infrastructure
- Agent API: benchmark score improved from 55% to 77% in roughly a month, cost down ~90%
- Internal adoption metric: 7x agent-hours per employee per week vs. September baseline
- Intel partnership: local models + hybrid inference targeting Intel Ultra Series 3 hardware (on-device compute path for personal/work agents)

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is a company using its own product internally and actually publishing the usage delta, which is rarer than it should be. "7x agent-hours per week" is a real adoption signal, not a launch press release, and it directly speaks to the open question of whether AI feature adoption follows different curves than traditional features. The Intel partnership is also worth watching: if local/hybrid inference becomes viable on consumer laptops, that changes the cost and latency calculus for any PM building agent features that currently assume cloud-only inference.

**Critical questions:**
- "Agent-hours per week" as a metric is vague, more usage could mean more value or just more retries and longer loops to get the same job done
- Is a 90% cost reduction from model efficiency gains, from routing to cheaper models for easier tasks, or from Perplexity simply eating margin to drive adoption numbers?
- Would this adoption rate hold outside a company whose own employees are maximally incentivized to use their own product?

**Action you could take today:** If you're tracking AI feature adoption internally, define an "hours saved" or "actions completed" metric now, before your next feature launch, so you have something other than raw usage counts to report.

### Notion - Rebuilt Its AI Pricing Around Credits, Not Seats

**Source:** https://www.notion.com/blog/notion-ai-more-for-less
**Credibility:** High (first-party product announcement with named engineering lead)

**What happened:** Notion shipped a reworked AI system: smarter model routing, long-running agents, a bring-your-own-AI option, and a new credit-based system replacing flat per-seat AI pricing. Co-founder Akshay Kothari frames it as "agents, rebuilt to do more for less." A companion engineering post from Ghita Houir Alami details the "Auto" model router, which uses pairwise comparisons and calibrated judging to pick models at scale rather than routing on static rules.

**Key technical details:**
- New credit-based pricing system replacing flat AI seat pricing
- "Auto" model router uses pairwise comparison + calibrated judging to select models per-request
- Long-running agents (not just single-turn chat responses)
- Bring-your-own-AI option, letting customers plug in their own model/API key instead of using Notion's bundled models
- Companion post: Notion connected its AI to 160 new apps via MCP in three weeks, described as "the secret was a Notion agent" building the integrations itself

**Why it matters for PMs:** Credit-based pricing for AI features is becoming the default pattern (see also Vercel's "Agent Sessions now cost 67% less" from earlier this month), because flat seat pricing doesn't map to the wildly variable cost of agent workloads where one task might cost pennies and another might run a long agent loop for an hour. The bring-your-own-AI option is the more interesting signal: Notion is unbundling the model from the product, which is a hedge against both cost unpredictability and the risk of being locked into one model provider's pricing and quality curve.

**Critical questions:**
- Does a credit system create user anxiety ("will I run out?") that flat pricing didn't, and does that suppress exactly the exploratory usage Notion wants to encourage?
- If bring-your-own-AI takes off, what happens to Notion's AI margin, and does that undercut the whole "do more for less" pitch?
- Using an agent to build 160 app integrations in three weeks is a notable dev-velocity claim. What's the quality bar on those integrations, and who tests them?

**Action you could take today:** If your product still prices AI on flat seats, model what a credit system would do to your top 10% of power users' bills, that's usually where the pricing model breaks first.

### Amjad Masad - The Origin Story of Vibe Coding

**Source:** https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/replit-vibe-coding-origin-story (via YC "How to Build the Future" interview with Paul Graham)
**Credibility:** Medium (founder interview, retrospective account, not independently verified)

**What happened:** In a wide-ranging interview with Paul Graham, Replit CEO Amjad Masad described how vibe coding emerged: Replit noticed users were pasting ChatGPT-generated code directly into the IDE, which led the team to build an AI agent that closed that gap directly inside the product. Separately, Masad posted on X that "Replit is the only collaborative AI coding system out there," responding to a request for team features in Claude Code.

**Key capabilities:**
- The insight came from observing an existing user workaround (copy-pasting from ChatGPT) rather than from a roadmap decision
- Replit built the agent to remove the copy-paste step entirely, positioning it as the connective tissue between "I have an idea" and "I have running code"
- Masad is positioning collaborative/team-based AI coding as Replit's differentiator against single-player tools like Claude Code

**Why it matters for PMs:** This is a clean, concrete example of a pattern every PM claims to use but rarely gets credit for: watching what users do around your product's edges, not just inside it. The copy-paste behavior was the signal; the agent was the response. It's also a useful data point for the build-vs-buy and differentiation open questions: Masad's bet is that collaboration, not raw coding capability, is where Replit can still win against better-funded single-player tools.

**Critical questions:**
- Is "collaborative AI coding" actually a defensible moat, or is it a feature gap that Claude Code and Cursor close within two quarters?
- How much of vibe coding's origin story is retrospective narrative-building versus an accurate account of the actual product decision process at the time?
- If the real insight was "watch the workaround," what workarounds are happening around your own product right now that you haven't built for yet?

**Action you could take today:** Pull your support tickets or community forum from the last 30 days and search for any mention of users pasting output from one tool into another, that handoff is usually a feature you haven't built yet.

## The Thread

**The AI pricing model is converging on usage-based credits, and the model choice is being unbundled from the product.** Notion's new credit system and bring-your-own-AI option, Vercel's cheaper agent sessions, and Perplexity's near-90% cost reduction on its Agent API all point the same direction: flat per-seat AI pricing doesn't survive contact with agent workloads, and the companies ahead of the curve are rebuilding billing around actual compute consumed rather than headcount.
